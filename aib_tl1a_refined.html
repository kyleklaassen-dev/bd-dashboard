  1. Protect clinical-entry speed — the p19-bispecific clinic is nearly empty (MT-251 Ph1; rest preclinical). The window is the asset.

2 · The scientific thesis (the case in four steps)

1) Engagement ≠ efficacy [F]. Anti-TL1A suppresses soluble TL1A ~90% yet delivers only ~26-28% remission. The drug does its molecular job; the disease continues.2

2) Why [I]. The gap is IL-23-independent Th17 activation and epithelial-restitution failure TL1A doesn't touch. The anti-TL1A (~72%) and anti-IL-23 (~55%) non-responder escape maps barely overlap — the mechanistic definition of a combination.3

3) Why p19 [I]. IL-23p19 closes the Th17 escape while preserving IL-12/Th1 immunity and inheriting the risankizumab class precedent (p40 adds redundancy + safety baggage).4

4) The testable conclusion [P]. Closing both non-overlapping pathways should convert engagement into remission — the 30-40% deep-remission hypothesis. Falsifiable: if dual blockade (RO7837195, MT-251 first) doesn't beat the best monospecific, the thesis fails. The single most important experiment in this area — not yet reported.
