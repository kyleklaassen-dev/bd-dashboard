IBD · TL1A × IL-23p19
Composite Estimand in IBD Trials
The composite estimand counts rescue medication use as treatment failure — even if a patient improved clinically. TL1A trials (ATLAS, STARSCAPE) all use this. Means the "true" remission bar is higher than older trials. Know this before benchmarking competitors.
Respiratory · TSLP × IL-33
TSLP as the Upstream Alarmin
TSLP sits at the top of the Type 2 cascade — released by epithelial cells in response to damage. Blocking TSLP prevents activation of ILC2, mast cells, Th2, and basophils simultaneously. This "upstream" position is why TSLP bispecifics pair more naturally with IL-33 (another alarmin) vs just IL-13.
Type 2 Inflammation · IL-4Rα
Why IL-4Rα Bispecifics Extend Dupixent
Dupixent blocks IL-4Rα (shared receptor for IL-4 and IL-13). Pairing with OX40L adds a T-cell costimulation blockade arm — targeting memory Th2 cell survival, not just acute cytokines. This is the rationale for IL-4Rα × OX40L in refractory AD: hitting both the cytokine and the cell that produces it.
Thyroid Eye Disease · IGF1R × TSHR
The IGF1R–TSHR Receptor Cross-Talk
In TED, IGF1R and TSHR form a physical complex on orbital fibroblasts. TSH-stimulating antibodies (TRAbs) activate TSHR, which transactivates IGF1R — triggering fibroblast proliferation and GAG deposition. Blocking both breaks the cross-talk loop that neither monotherapy fully addresses alone.
Autoimmune · FcRn
How FcRn Recycling Extends Half-Life
FcRn (neonatal Fc receptor) rescues IgG from lysosomal degradation. Endosomes at pH 6 capture IgG via FcRn; at cell surface (pH 7.4) IgG is released back to circulation. Albumin uses the same receptor. A bispecific with an albumin VHH binder leverages albumin's FcRn recycling to dramatically extend half-life — enabling monthly or quarterly dosing.
Immune Reset · BCMA × CD19 × CD3
Why Target Both CD19 and BCMA?
CD19 marks all B cells including naïve and memory cells in circulation. BCMA marks long-lived plasma cells in the bone marrow — the cells that maintain autoantibody titers for years and are invisible to CD19 monotherapy. Combining CD19 + BCMA + CD3 (trispecific TCE) eliminates the full B-cell lineage, enabling a true immune reset in diseases like SLE, myositis, and systemic sclerosis.

Estimand Intelligence — Why the Same Endpoint Can Mean Different Things
ICH E9(R1) · Critical for interpreting IBD remission rates across TL1A trials
Essential BD Context ▼
An estimand defines the exact clinical question a trial is trying to answer — not just the endpoint, but how the trial handles real-world disruptions like rescue medication use, dose changes, discontinuations, and dropouts. Two IBD trials can both report "Week 52 remission" while answering fundamentally different questions. ICH E9(R1) (2019) requires sponsors to pre-specify the estimand before the trial starts.
Composite
Most common in IBD. Intercurrent events (rescue steroids, prohibited meds, discontinuation) are incorporated into the outcome — patients who need rescue are counted as non-responders, regardless of their actual Week 52 status. Measures real-world treatment success including tolerability. Industry standard for UC/CD Phase 2b/3.
Treatment Policy
ITT regardless of intercurrent events. All patients analyzed based on original assignment, whatever happened — rescue meds, dose changes, discontinuation. Measures the drug's effect in practice, not under ideal conditions. More common in respiratory (exacerbation trials) than IBD. Can inflate apparent remission rates if patients got rescue.
Hypothetical
Ideal efficacy if the intercurrent event hadn't occurred. Estimates what remission would look like if no patient needed rescue or switched therapy. Useful for mechanism of action understanding and dose-finding. Rarely used as primary estimand in late-stage IBD; can produce higher, less clinically meaningful efficacy numbers.
While on Treatment
Outcome measured only while patient stays on therapy. Excludes post-discontinuation outcomes. Relevant for understanding pharmacological effect without confounding from treatment switch. Underestimates real-world effectiveness for chronic diseases with high discontinuation; rarely used as primary in IBD.
BD read-through for IBD: When comparing TL1A remission rates across tulisokibart (Merck), duvakitug (Teva/Sanofi), and afimkibart (Roche), verify that all three are using a composite estimand with the same intercurrent event definition. A composite-based 35% remission rate is not comparable to a treatment-policy-based 45% remission rate — the second number is answering a different, easier question. This is particularly critical when comparing monotherapy context data to inform bispecific design assumptions. Future TL1A × IL-23p19 Phase 2 trials should pre-specify composite estimands to align with the emerging IBD standard.

Biology Deep-Dive: TL1A / DR3 · IBD Pathophysiology · MOA Framework

▼
What is TL1A and Why Does It Matter?
TL1A (TNF-like Ligand 1A, also called TNFSF15) is a cytokine belonging to the TNF superfamily. It is expressed primarily by vascular endothelial cells, monocytes, macrophages, and dendritic cells when those cells encounter inflammatory signals such as bacterial products, TLR ligands, or other cytokines. TL1A signals through its cognate receptor, Death Receptor 3 (DR3, TNFRSF25), which is constitutively expressed on T lymphocytes — particularly CD4+ effector T cells, regulatory T cells (Tregs), and memory T cells. This constitutive expression means the system is "always ready" to respond, and even subtle TL1A upregulation in inflamed tissue will trigger T cell activation.
The key insight: TL1A is not just another inflammatory cytokine — it is a T cell co-stimulatory signal that amplifies responses to other activating stimuli. When IL-12 or IL-18 activates T cells, TL1A amplifies that signal 10-100x. In the context of IBD, where T cell dysfunction is central, TL1A acts as a "volume knob" for intestinal inflammation.
In the gut mucosa specifically, TL1A-DR3 signaling drives the expansion and activation of Th9, Th17, Th1, and ILC3 populations — the exact inflammatory cell subsets most relevant in both UC and CD. It also has a complex role in regulatory T cells: TL1A can both suppress Tregs (removing a brake on inflammation) or, in different contexts, promote Treg expansion — which is why patient selection and biomarker strategies are so important in TL1A drug development.
TL1A in IBD: Disease Biology
Multiple lines of evidence support TL1A as a causal driver in IBD, not merely a bystander:
Genetic evidence: Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have identified the TNFSF15 locus as one of the strongest genetic risk factors for Crohn's disease. The risk haplotype is associated with TL1A overexpression in response to inflammatory stimuli. Approximately 60% of IBD patients carry a TNFSF15 risk variant — this is the scientific basis for Merck's companion diagnostic strategy with tulisokibart.
Expression data: TL1A protein is significantly upregulated in the inflamed mucosa of both UC and CD patients compared to healthy controls and non-inflamed IBD tissue. The level of mucosal TL1A expression correlates with disease severity and histological inflammation scores.
Functional studies: TL1A-transgenic mice develop a Th2/Th9-driven colitis with small intestinal fibrosis — importantly capturing fibrotic features of Crohn's disease that are often absent in other models. Anti-TL1A antibodies show efficacy in multiple mouse colitis models including DSS and T-cell transfer models.
Why this matters for BD: A target with strong genetic + expression + functional validation is significantly de-risked compared to targets with only one type of evidence. TL1A has all three, which explains why multiple major pharma companies have committed billions in the space.
The TL1A × IL-23 Synergy — Why Bispecifics?
IL-23 (specifically the p19 subunit, targeted by risankizumab, guselkumab, and others) is the master driver of Th17 and ILC3 differentiation in the gut. It promotes production of IL-17A, IL-17F, and IL-22 — cytokines that disrupt the epithelial barrier and sustain chronic inflammation in CD. IL-23 is already a validated target: risankizumab (Skyrizi) is approved for both CD and UC with strong efficacy data.
The key mechanistic connection: TL1A and IL-23 act on overlapping populations (DR3 is expressed on Th17 cells, ILC3s, and other IL-23-responsive cells) and co-stimulate in a synergistic, non-redundant fashion. Studies show that when both TL1A and IL-23 are present, the inflammatory response is greater than either alone — and that blocking one pathway leaves the other active to maintain inflammation.
BD Evaluation Lens: Why the Bispecific Premium?
The bispecific rationale is straightforward but must be validated clinically: if dual blockade achieves meaningfully higher remission rates than either monoAb alone (and both are approved individually), a bispecific can command a premium label, pricing, and formulary position. The AbbVie TARGET-CD trial (risankizumab + trosunilimab combination, H2 2026 readout) is the most direct validation of this hypothesis — if positive, it would be the strongest endorsement for bispecific programs. When evaluating bispecific programs, ask: (1) What are the expected additive vs. synergistic effects? (2) Which estimand design maximizes the detectable benefit? (3) Is there IND-stage efficacy data showing the bispecific outperforms both monoAbs?
IBD Drug Development: Endpoints, Estimands & Patient Selection
Endpoints: In UC, the standard primary endpoint for pivotal trials is clinical remission, defined by Mayo score (stool frequency, rectal bleeding, endoscopy). Composite remission requires both clinical and endoscopic remission. In CD, endoscopic response/remission and CDAI-based endpoints are used. Regulators increasingly prefer endoscopic endpoints as they reflect mucosal healing and predict long-term outcomes.
Why estimands matter: The composite estimand counts patients who required rescue medication as "treatment failures," creating a more rigorous but accurate picture of drug performance in real-world use. The treatment policy estimand counts all events regardless of rescue medication changes — more reflective of real-world outcomes but can make drugs look better. Regulators now favor alignment with ICH E9(R1) guidance. Comparing tulisokibart (26.5%, composite) vs. duvakitug (48%, composite) requires understanding whether both trials defined composite remission identically.
Patient selection: Biologic-naive vs. biologic-experienced patients respond very differently to anti-TL1A agents. Biologic-experienced patients (who have failed anti-TNF or anti-integrin) tend to have more refractory disease and lower response rates. Companion diagnostics (TNFSF15 haplotype) can enrich for responders, but a more restrictive label limits commercial opportunity.

Estimand Intelligence — Why Exacerbation Rates Are Not Always Comparable
ICH E9(R1) · Critical context for interpreting TSLP and IL-33 respiratory trial data
Essential BD Context ▼
Respiratory trials for TSLP and IL-33 inhibitors predominantly use a treatment policy estimand — they count exacerbations regardless of whether patients changed ICS doses, used rescue OCS, or discontinued the study drug. This is why tezepelumab (NAVIGATOR) could demonstrate efficacy across all asthma endotypes even in patients who adjusted background therapy. However, different trials handle OCS reduction, COPD exacerbation definitions, and hospitalization severity thresholds differently — making cross-trial comparisons treacherous without understanding the exact estimand.
Treatment Policy
Standard for exacerbation endpoints in asthma/COPD. Counts all exacerbations regardless of background therapy changes or rescue med use. Pragmatic — reflects real-world use. Most approved TSLP/IL-33 assets use this for primary endpoint. Tezepelumab (NAVIGATOR), itepekimab (QUEST/LIBERTY), tozorakimab (RESCUE-COPD), astegolimab all use treatment policy as primary.
Composite
Used for OCS reduction and steroid-sparing endpoints. If a patient escalates OCS (intercurrent event), it's incorporated into the composite outcome as treatment failure. Common for atopic dermatitis and severe asthma OCS tapering trials. Bispecific TSLP×IL-33 assets targeting COPD may encounter composite estimands when exacerbation-triggered hospitalization is incorporated into the primary outcome.
Hypothetical
What would happen if background therapy hadn't changed? Rarely used as primary in respiratory. Sometimes used as secondary to understand the drug's mechanism independent of ICS/OCS adjustments. Useful for Phase 2 dose-finding in COPD, where patients frequently adjust maintenance therapy. Can produce optimistic efficacy estimates not replicable in broader populations.
While on Treatment
Annualized exacerbation rate while receiving study drug. Used in trials with planned treatment cessation or long wash-out periods. Underestimates benefit if exacerbation risk rebounds post-discontinuation (relevant for biologics with extended half-lives, like long-acting TSLP inhibitors). Qyuns' long-acting bispecific QX031N may require specific consideration here given its extended PK profile.
BD read-through for Respiratory: Tezepelumab's landmark "works across all endotypes" story is only valid under its treatment policy estimand — patients whose ICS doses changed mid-trial were still counted, making the result more pragmatic. If a TSLP×IL-33 bispecific uses a different estimand — for example, a hypothetical estimand that excludes patients who escalated ICS — it may report better-looking efficacy numbers that aren't comparable to tezepelumab's NAVIGATOR data. Additionally, COPD exacerbation definitions vary significantly (Type 1: antibiotics only; Type 2: OCS; Type 3: hospitalization) — trials using different severity thresholds are not cross-comparable even if both use treatment policy estimands. Chinese companies designing TSLP bispecific Phase 2 protocols for COPD should align their exacerbation definition and estimand with the tezepelumab NAVIGATOR standard to maximize regulatory and partner credibility.

1 · What Are TSLP and IL-33 — and Why Do They Matter?

TSLP (Thymic Stromal Lymphopoietin) and IL-33 are epithelial alarmins — danger signals released by barrier tissues (skin, lung, gut mucosa) in response to physical damage, allergens, pollutants, viruses, and cigarette smoke. They act upstream of the adaptive immune response, functioning as a first-line broadcast to the immune system that the epithelial barrier has been breached or stressed.

TSLP is a cytokine of the IL-2 superfamily. It signals through a heterodimeric receptor (TSLPR + IL-7Rα) expressed on dendritic cells, mast cells, basophils, innate lymphoid cells (ILC2s), and T cells. When epithelial cells release TSLP, dendritic cells rapidly upregulate OX40L, a co-stimulatory signal that drives naïve T cells toward a Th2 phenotype — the cellular hallmark of atopic disease. TSLP also directly activates ILC2s, triggering the production of IL-5 and IL-13, which in turn drive eosinophilia and airway remodeling. Because TSLP sits so far upstream — before the Th2 cascade even begins — blocking it should theoretically interrupt all downstream type 2 inflammation, not just one arm of it.

IL-33 is a member of the IL-1 family stored as a nuclear chromatin-associated protein. Upon cell death, necrosis, or mechanical stress, IL-33 is released and signals through ST2 (IL1RL1) on mast cells, ILC2s, Th2 cells, eosinophils, and regulatory T cells. Like TSLP, IL-33 robustly activates ILC2s and amplifies type 2 inflammation — but its receptor distribution also gives it effects in cardiac tissue, the gut, and the CNS. Critically, IL-33 and TSLP signal through distinct receptors and partially non-redundant downstream pathways: TSLP preferentially activates dendritic cells and the Th2 polarization axis, while IL-33 most potently activates mast cells and ILC2s in both type 2 and non-type 2 contexts.

Why Block Both?

Single-cytokine blockade leaves escape valves open. Tezepelumab (anti-TSLP mono) delivered landmark results — but its Phase 3 COPD data are pending, and there are still ~40% of patients who don't respond. IL-33 blockade (tozorakimab, itepekimab) adds complementary efficacy, especially in COPD where non-type 2 inflammation driven by IL-33 upon cigarette smoke-induced cell death may be the dominant pathology. A TSLP×IL-33 bispecific should theoretically achieve deeper, broader suppression of alarmin-driven inflammation across both the Th2 and non-Th2 axes simultaneously — with one molecule and one injection.

2 · Respiratory Disease Biology: Asthma vs. COPD — Why They're Different

Asthma and COPD are often mentioned in the same breath (literally), but their biology, patient populations, and regulatory paths are fundamentally different. Understanding this distinction is essential for evaluating respiratory biologic programs.

Asthma is a heterogeneous inflammatory airway disease characterized by reversible bronchoconstriction and airway hyperresponsiveness. The dominant endotype in severe asthma is type 2 high (T2-high), defined by elevated blood or sputum eosinophils (≥150–300 cells/μL), elevated IgE, periostin, or FeNO >25 ppb. However, ~40–50% of severe asthma is type 2 low, characterized by neutrophilic or paucigranulocytic inflammation — and most existing biologics fail here. Tezepelumab's key differentiation in asthma is that it works regardless of baseline eosinophil count, implying TSLP-driven pathways beyond pure T2 inflammation. Clinical endpoint: annualized asthma exacerbation rate (AAER), with FEV₁ improvement and OCS reduction as key secondaries.

COPD is fundamentally different. It is a progressive, largely irreversible obstructive disease caused primarily by cigarette smoke exposure, characterized by emphysema and small airway remodeling. The dominant inflammatory pathology involves macrophages, neutrophils, and CD8+ T cells — a largely non-type 2 picture. However, a significant minority of COPD patients have eosinophilic inflammation (blood eos ≥300 is the key threshold for dupilumab's COPD label), and these patients respond to type 2 biologics. IL-33 is especially relevant in COPD because it is released upon cell necrosis from cigarette-smoke-induced epithelial damage, activating mast cells and macrophages in ways that may not depend on type 2 adaptive immunity. This is why itepekimab (anti-IL-33) showed benefit in ex-smoker COPD with eos ≥150, and why TSLP×IL-33 bispecifics may be particularly interesting in this indication. Clinical endpoint: annualized rate of moderate-to-severe AECOPD (acute exacerbations), with FEV₁ and St. George's Respiratory Questionnaire (SGRQ) as key secondaries. Regulatory note: COPD label claims typically require two positive Phase 3 trials across distinct exacerbation-prone populations.

Why COPD Is the Market Prize

Asthma is increasingly well-served (dupilumab, tezepelumab, benralizumab, mepolizumab). COPD has ~380 million patients globally, enormous unmet need, limited approved biologics (only dupilumab as of 2024), and peak sales potential estimated at $10B+ per year for a successful entrant. This is why every major respiratory BD deal — QX031N/Roche ($1.07B), Windward/Kelun/Harbour, tozorakimab/Zenith — includes explicit COPD development plans.

3 · TSLP × IL-33 Bispecifics — The Scientific and Commercial Rationale

The bispecific hypothesis rests on three observations from the monospecific data: (1) tezepelumab reduces AAER by ~70% in T2-high asthma but has limited demonstrated effect in COPD to date; (2) itepekimab reduces AECOPDs in ex-smoker COPD, suggesting IL-33 drives exacerbation pathology through a different mechanism than TSLP; (3) combination anti-TSLP + anti-IL-33 in animal models shows additive-to-synergistic effects on both epithelial barrier repair and ILC2 suppression.

Mechanistically, the dual blockade approach addresses the two key failure modes of each monospecific. Anti-TSLP alone doesn't adequately suppress IL-33-driven mast cell activation (which contributes to both acute bronchoconstriction and exacerbations). Anti-IL-33 alone doesn't interrupt TSLP-driven Th2 polarization (which is especially important in allergen-triggered asthma and in patients with atopic comorbidity). A single molecule that neutralizes both upstream alarmins should provide broader coverage of the ILC2/mast cell/Th2 axis simultaneously.

From a biology standpoint, there is also a receptor crosstalk argument: TSLP and IL-33 both converge on ILC2 activation via NF-κB and GATA3 pathways. Blocking both inputs may be required to achieve full ILC2 quiescence — blocking either one alone may leave residual ILC2 activation. This parallels the dual IL-4/IL-13 blockade rationale for dupilumab vs. mono IL-13 blockade (tralokinumab, lebrikizumab).

Key Unknowns Heading Into Phase 2

Does dual blockade actually outperform tezepelumab monotherapy in asthma? The comparator bar is very high — 70% AAER reduction is exceptional. In COPD, does adding TSLP inhibition to IL-33 blockade improve on itepekimab's ~25% AECOPD reduction in ex-smokers? And which biomarker subgroups drive efficacy — high eosinophils, high IgE, ex-smoker status, or FeNO? These questions will define Phase 2/3 trial design and the asset's differentiation story at partnering.

4 · Respiratory Drug Development: Endpoints, Biomarkers & Trial Design

The canonical Phase 3 asthma endpoint is annualized asthma exacerbation rate (AAER) over 52 weeks in patients with ≥2 exacerbations in the prior year despite medium-to-high dose ICS ± LABA. The primary estimand choice matters enormously. The treatment policy estimand counts all exacerbations regardless of rescue medication use or background therapy changes — this is what tezepelumab's NAVIGATOR used, and it underpinned the "all-endotype" label claim. A hypothetical estimand (what would happen if patients stayed on treatment without rescue?) might show larger effect sizes but doesn't reflect real-world clinical practice and creates labeling risk.

Key asthma biomarkers for enrollment and subgroup analysis:

  • Blood eosinophils — ≥150 (low-threshold), ≥300 (high T2). Most anti-IL-5/IL-5R biologics require ≥300. Tezepelumab works down to 0.
  • FeNO (fractional exhaled nitric oxide) — ≥25 ppb indicates airway eosinophilic inflammation; ≥50 ppb = highly correlated with response to anti-IgE and anti-IL-4/13.
  • Total serum IgE — elevated IgE correlates with atopic phenotype; relevant for dupilumab and omalizumab subgroups.
  • Periostin — epithelial IL-13-driven marker; used in lebrikizumab trials but less common now.
  • Allergen sensitization — important for allergen-challenge models and for differentiating allergic from non-allergic asthma.

COPD trial design fundamentals: The primary endpoint is moderate-to-severe AECOPD rate over 52 weeks in patients with GOLD 2-4 severity and ≥2 exacerbations in the prior year (or 1 hospitalization). FEV₁ reversibility is typically ≤12% (fixed obstruction). Key biomarker thresholds: blood eos ≥150 (dupilumab used ≥300; itepekimab used ≥150 in ex-smokers). ICH E9(R1) estimand considerations apply: whether exacerbations during hospitalization are included, how deaths are handled, and whether time-to-first-exacerbation analyses provide supportive evidence all need to be pre-specified.

BD Evaluation Lens — Respiratory Programs

When evaluating a TSLP×IL-33 or related alarmin-targeting asset for partnership, the key diligence questions are: (1) Which estimand was used in Phase 2? Treatment policy or hypothetical? How does the effect size hold up under the more conservative assumption? (2) What is the eosinophil distribution in their enrolled cohort? High-eos enrichment inflates Phase 2 results in a way that may not replicate in a broader Phase 3 population. (3) Is there a COPD signal? Given the commercial stakes, an asset that only shows asthma data is substantially less valuable than one with even early COPD signals. (4) How does the bispecific format affect PK? Half-life, dosing interval (Q4W vs Q8W), and SC deliverability all influence patient access and commercial positioning. (5) What happens to TSLP and IL-33 levels post-dosing? Target engagement biomarkers confirm MOA and de-risk later-stage failures. Ask for TSLPR occupancy data and free vs. total cytokine levels. (6) How differentiated is this vs. tezepelumab? The approval bar in asthma is now 70% AAER reduction with broad labeling. A new entrant needs to match that or target a subpopulation (e.g., T2-low, severe COPD) where tezepelumab hasn't demonstrated benefit.

TED Pathophysiology — IGF1R–TSHR Cross-Talk on Orbital Fibroblasts
Why teprotumumab works, why it's incomplete, and why dual receptor blockade is the rational next step
Disease Education▼
Thyroid Eye Disease (TED) is an autoimmune orbital disorder occurring primarily in patients with Graves' disease. The disease driver is TSH receptor autoantibodies (TRAbs) which stimulate the TSHR on orbital fibroblasts. Critically, TSHR and IGF-1R exist in a physical receptor complex on these fibroblasts — TRAb stimulation of TSHR leads to transactivation of IGF-1R, triggering fibroblast proliferation, glycosaminoglycan (GAG) deposition, and the clinical features of TED (proptosis, diplopia, periorbital inflammation, compressive optic neuropathy). Teprotumumab proved that blocking IGF-1R alone achieves dramatic clinical benefit, but the TSHR continues to signal through alternative pathways — explaining the ~40% relapse rate. Dual blockade of both IGF-1R and TSHR severs both arms of the co-receptor complex simultaneously.
TSHR
TSH Receptor — expressed on orbital fibroblasts. Bound by TRAbs (pathologic IgG autoantibodies) in Graves' disease. TSHR stimulation initiates the fibroblast activation cascade. Continues to signal even when IGF-1R is blocked by teprotumumab.
IGF-1R
Insulin-like Growth Factor Receptor — co-expressed with TSHR on orbital fibroblasts. TRAb stimulation of TSHR transactivates IGF-1R independently of IGF-1 ligand. Teprotumumab blocks this arm → dramatic proptosis reduction (83% responders). But TSHR pathway remains open.
Cross-Talk
The two receptors form a physical complex (Pritchard/Smith group). Blocking only one arm leaves the other available for signaling. This receptor cross-talk explains why: (1) teprotumumab works (IGF-1R contributes meaningfully to fibroblast activation), and (2) ~40% relapse (TSHR continues to drive the fibroblast pool long-term).
Dual Blockade
IGF1R × TSHR bispecific severs both pathways simultaneously. Expected benefit: deeper acute proptosis reduction AND prevention of relapse by blocking the TSHR-driven maintenance signal. SC target + cleaner safety profile (reduced IGF-1R dose enabled by TSHR co-blockade = less systemic IGF-1R inhibition = potentially less hearing loss).

FcRn Biology — How IgG Recycling Works & Why Blocking It Depletes Autoantibodies
The mechanism behind the entire FcRn inhibitor class and how Ailux's albumin VHH extends it
Disease Education▼
The neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) is expressed on endothelial and hematopoietic cells. It acts as a salvage receptor that rescues IgG antibodies from lysosomal degradation — extending the natural IgG half-life from approximately 7 days to the clinically observed ~21 days. The mechanism: IgG taken up by cells via pinocytosis is captured by FcRn in the acidic endosome (pH 6.0) through high-affinity Fc binding; when the endosome fuses with the cell surface (pH 7.4), the affinity drops and the IgG is released back into circulation. FcRn inhibitors block this rescue mechanism — IgG is degraded in the lysosome instead of recycled, reducing total IgG including pathologic autoantibodies by 60–80%. Critically, this mechanism is IgG-specific: IgM, IgA, and IgE use different recycling pathways and are largely unaffected, which is why FcRn inhibitors have a cleaner immune suppression profile than broad immunosuppressants. Albumin uses the same FcRn receptor — Ailux's albumin VHH binder hitches the FcRn inhibitor molecule onto albumin's FcRn-mediated recycling, dramatically extending the bispecific's half-life beyond what Fc engineering alone achieves.
FcRn Recycling
pH 6 endosome: FcRn binds IgG Fc with high affinity → IgG rescued from lysosome. pH 7.4 surface: affinity drops → IgG released to circulation. FcRn inhibitors block this capture → IgG degraded. Result: 60–80% IgG reduction including pathologic autoantibodies.
IgG Selectivity
FcRn is specific to IgG and albumin. IgM, IgA, IgE are not recycled by FcRn → preserved during FcRn inhibition. This means vaccines (IgG-mediated) are partially impacted but mucosal immunity (IgA) and allergy protection (IgE) are preserved. Cleaner profile than IVIG or plasmapheresis.
Albumin VHH
Albumin (19-day natural half-life) uses FcRn for its own recycling — same mechanism as IgG. Ailux's VHH binder attaches to albumin → the entire FcRn inhibitor molecule rides albumin's FcRn recycling. This dramatically extends the PK beyond what Fc engineering alone achieves, enabling Q6–8W dosing vs. Q1W for conventional FcRn mAbs.
Target Diseases
Any IgG autoantibody-driven disease. Highest-value: gMG (AChR-IgG), pemphigus vulgaris (DSG1/3-IgG), NMOSD (AQP4-IgG), ITP (anti-platelet IgG), CIDP (anti-nodal IgG), WAIHA, warm AIHA, IgG4-RD, SLE. 20+ active indications across the class.

ACE Biology — B-Cell Lineage, BCMA, CD19 & the Immune Reset Hypothesis
Understanding why dual depletion achieves what neither CD19 nor BCMA alone can
Disease Education▼
Autoantibody-driven diseases (SLE, gMG, SSc, Sjögren's) are maintained by two distinct populations: short-lived plasmablasts that express CD19 and replenish continuously, and long-lived plasma cells in the bone marrow that are CD19−/BCMA+ and can survive for years. Rituximab depletes circulating B cells (CD20+) but cannot reach bone marrow plasma cells. CD19 CAR-T depletes more thoroughly but still misses CD19− plasma cells. BCMA×CD3 alone clears bone marrow plasma cells but not circulating B cells. ACE (BCMA×CD19×CD3) is the first approach designed to deplete both compartments simultaneously — delivering the comprehensive immune reset that CAR-T approaches suggest is curative in autoimmune disease.
CD19+ B Cells
Circulating B cells, memory B cells, plasmablasts. CD19 arm engages T cells to kill this compartment. Blinatumomab and CD19 CAR-T both work here. Replenished rapidly — need persistent depletion to allow immune re-tolerization.
BCMA+ Plasma Cells
Long-lived bone marrow plasma cells. CD19−/BCMA+. These are the persistent antibody factories not reached by rituximab or CD19 CAR-T alone. Teclistamab (BCMA×CD3) targets this compartment — NEJM 2024 remission in SLE validates the approach.
Immune Re-Tolerization
Hypothesis: depleting both B-cell and plasma cell compartments creates a window during B-cell reconstitution in which autoreactive clones fail to re-emerge and the immune system re-tolerizes to self. Durability depends on depth and completeness of depletion — the rationale for dual targeting.
CRS Risk Mitigation
CD3 affinity is intentionally attenuated (sub-micromolar) in next-gen TCEs to reduce peak cytokine release. Step-up dosing, pre-medication with dexamethasone, and tocilizumab rescue are the established safety framework from oncology.

Market Signal Framework — How to Predict Moves Before They Happen
The biotech market is not random. Clinical catalysts, deal precedents, and regulatory signals are learnable patterns. This section teaches you to read them.
Rule 1: How to Read a Phase 2 Readout Before the Market Does ▼
The market prices in 60–70% of expected outcome by the day of readout. Most of the move happens in the weeks before — driven by conference abstracts, trial enrollment updates, and sell-side analyst previews. If you're waiting for the press release to trade, you're last.
Read the design, not just the endpoint. A Phase 2 trial that uses a composite estimand will report higher remission rates than one using a treatment policy estimand. Duvakitug's 48% Ph2b remission (composite) may look very different in Ph3 under a pragmatic treatment policy design. Watch for this translation risk when evaluating readout expectations.
Primary miss ≠ death. Afimkibart missed its Ph2b primary endpoint but Roche still advanced to Ph3. Why? Secondary endpoints were positive and biomarker-positive subgroups showed meaningful signal. When a drug misses primary but secondaries hold, watch for: (1) stock selloff on day of miss, (2) gradual recovery as analysts parse subgroup data, (3) re-rating when the company announces Ph3 plans with refined population. This is a common pattern in IBD and respiratory biologics.
The p-value isn't everything — context is. A p=0.08 in a noisy indication like COPD with a positive directional trend and a validated biomarker subgroup (eos ≥150) can still advance. A p=0.04 in a clean UC trial with a weak effect size may not. Learn to read the full data package: magnitude of effect, biomarker enrichment, consistency across secondary endpoints, and estimand choice.
Rule 2: Deal Economics as a Signal — What Upfronts Tell You ▼
Upfront as % of total = partner conviction. A $125M upfront on a $1.85B deal (Earendil/Sanofi) = 6.7%. A $75M upfront on a $1.07B deal (Qyuns/Roche) = 7.0%. These are preclinical assets — partners are paying real cash up front because the data package (preclinical efficacy, format, IP) is de-risked. Watch this ratio. If upfronts drop below 3-4% at preclinical stage in this class, sentiment is shifting.
Deal velocity matters. Three TL1A×IL-23p19 deals in under 12 months (Earendil/Sanofi Apr 2025, Qyuns/Roche Oct 2025, Simcere/BI Jan 2026) signals a supply shortage of high-quality bispecific assets. When partners are racing each other, they overpay. The next deal in this class will likely set a new upfront record — watch for it.
Who retains China rights tells you who has the leverage. Every major Chinese biotech in this space is retaining Greater China rights while licensing global ex-China. This is not a negotiating failure — it's a deliberate strategy that is working. If a Chinese company starts licensing global including China, it signals they either need cash urgently or the asset failed domestically.
Stock impact of deals: upfront is what moves the stock. Biotech stock algorithms watch for the upfront payment announcement, not the total potential value. A $50M upfront is real money today; $900M in milestones is mostly hypothetical. The market discounts milestones at 80–90%. So when reading a deal announcement, mentally note the upfront and subtract 85% from the biobucks to get the market's likely reaction.
Rule 3: Pipeline Catalysts That Move Adjacent Stocks ▼
A positive readout in one drug lifts all ships in the class — briefly. When tulisokibart ATLAS-UC reads out (~Nov 2026), positive data will lift: Roche (afimkibart), Sanofi (duvakitug), and AstraZeneca broadly. It also validates the bispecific thesis and likely triggers a search for unlicensed bispecific assets. This is when deal velocity in bispecifics will accelerate.
A Phase 3 failure re-prices the whole class downward. If ATLAS-UC misses, expect: (1) Sanofi/Teva stock to drop on duvakitug concerns despite better Ph2 data; (2) deal appetite for new TL1A assets to freeze for 3–6 months; (3) analyst rewrites on "TL1A class risk" that will unfairly tar bispecifics. This is when to be patient — the underlying science doesn't change and bispecifics may decouple from monospecifics in the narrative over 6–12 months.
COPD is the 2026–2027 inflection point for respiratory. Four readouts coming: Tozorakimab OBERON/MIRANDA (H2 2026), itepekimab AERIFY confirmation data, Bosakitug asthma (H2 2026), tezepelumab SUNRISE COPD (2026–2027). Each positive result reinforces the alarmin pathway story and adds commercial urgency for bispecifics. If SUNRISE (tezepelumab in COPD) is positive, AstraZeneca will have a new $10B product — and it will set the bar that QX031N must beat.
Rule 4: How to Evaluate a Chinese Biotech Asset for BD Potential ▼
Look for the bispecific format advantage first. Monospecific anti-TL1A and anti-TSLP are already licensed or approved. A new Chinese entrant needs to be a bispecific or a long-acting format to command premium deal economics. If the asset is a straight monoAb competing with tezepelumab or tulisokibart, the deal value will be significantly lower.
Phase 1 HV data is the first real gate. Before any serious BD conversation, partners want to see: (1) safety profile in humans, (2) PK confirming the expected half-life, (3) target engagement biomarkers (TSLP/IL-33 or TL1A suppression in serum). If a Chinese company doesn't have at least SAD/MAD Phase 1 data, they're in "optionality" territory — deals happen but at lower upfronts with higher milestone back-loading.
The China-retains-global-licenses pattern has an expiration date. As more assets get licensed, the remaining unlicensed bispecifics will command higher premiums. The window for Chinese companies to transact at preclinical stage is probably 2–3 more years. After Phase 3 data starts reading out in IBD (2026–2027), partners will have real efficacy data to work with and will demand Ph2 proof-of-concept before paying bispecific premiums.
