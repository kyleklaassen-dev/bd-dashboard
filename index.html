Biology Deep-Dive: TL1A / DR3 · IBD Pathophysiology · MOA Framework
What is TL1A and Why Does It Matter?
TL1A (TNF-like Ligand 1A, also called TNFSF15) is a cytokine belonging to the TNF superfamily. It is expressed primarily by vascular endothelial cells, monocytes, macrophages, and dendritic cells when those cells encounter inflammatory signals such as bacterial products, TLR ligands, or other cytokines. TL1A signals through its cognate receptor, Death Receptor 3 (DR3, TNFRSF25), which is constitutively expressed on T lymphocytes — particularly CD4+ effector T cells, regulatory T cells (Tregs), and memory T cells. This constitutive expression means the system is "always ready" to respond, and even subtle TL1A upregulation in inflamed tissue will trigger T cell activation.
The key insight: TL1A is not just another inflammatory cytokine — it is a T cell co-stimulatory signal that amplifies responses to other activating stimuli. When IL-12 or IL-18 activates T cells, TL1A amplifies that signal 10–100x. In the context of IBD, where T cell dysfunction is central, TL1A acts as a "volume knob" for intestinal inflammation.
In the gut mucosa specifically, TL1A-DR3 signaling drives the expansion and activation of Th9, Th17, Th1, and ILC3 populations — the exact inflammatory cell subsets most relevant in both UC and CD. It also has a complex role in regulatory T cells: TL1A can both suppress Tregs (removing a brake on inflammation) or, in different contexts, promote Treg expansion — which is why patient selection and biomarker strategies are so important in TL1A drug development.
TL1A in IBD: Disease Biology
Multiple lines of evidence support TL1A as a causal driver in IBD, not merely a bystander:
Genetic evidence: GWAS have identified the TNFSF15 locus as one of the strongest genetic risk factors for Crohn's disease. The risk haplotype is associated with TL1A overexpression. ~60% of IBD patients carry a TNFSF15 risk variant — the basis for Merck's companion diagnostic strategy.
Expression data: TL1A protein is significantly upregulated in the inflamed mucosa of both UC and CD patients. Mucosal TL1A expression correlates with disease severity and histological inflammation scores.
Functional studies: TL1A-transgenic mice develop a Th2/Th9-driven colitis with small intestinal fibrosis — capturing fibrotic features of Crohn's disease often absent in other models.
Why this matters for BD: A target with strong genetic + expression + functional validation is significantly de-risked. TL1A has all three, which explains why multiple major pharma companies have committed billions in the space.
The TL1A × IL-23 Synergy — Why Bispecifics?
IL-23 (specifically the p19 subunit) is the master driver of Th17 and ILC3 differentiation in the gut. It promotes IL-17A, IL-17F, and IL-22 — cytokines that disrupt the epithelial barrier and sustain chronic inflammation in CD. IL-23 is already a validated target: risankizumab (Skyrizi) is approved for both CD and UC with strong efficacy data.
The key mechanistic connection: TL1A and IL-23 act on overlapping populations (DR3 is expressed on Th17 cells, ILC3s, and other IL-23-responsive cells) and co-stimulate in a synergistic, non-redundant fashion. Studies show that when both TL1A and IL-23 are present, the inflammatory response is greater than either alone — and that blocking one pathway leaves the other active to maintain inflammation.
BD Evaluation Lens: Why the Bispecific Premium?
The bispecific rationale: if dual blockade achieves meaningfully higher remission rates than either monoAb alone, a bispecific can command a premium label, pricing, and formulary position. The AbbVie TARGET-CD trial (risankizumab + trosunilimab combination, H2 2026 readout) is the most direct validation of this hypothesis. When evaluating bispecific programs, ask: (1) What are the expected additive vs. synergistic effects? (2) Which estimand design maximizes the detectable benefit? (3) Is there IND-stage efficacy data showing the bispecific outperforms both monoAbs?
IBD Drug Development: Endpoints, Estimands & Patient Selection
Endpoints: In UC, the standard primary endpoint for pivotal trials is clinical remission, defined by Mayo score. Composite remission requires both clinical and endoscopic remission. In CD, endoscopic response/remission and CDAI-based endpoints are used. Regulators increasingly prefer endoscopic endpoints as they reflect mucosal healing and predict long-term outcomes.
Why estimands matter: The composite estimand counts patients who required rescue medication as "treatment failures," creating a rigorous picture of drug performance in real-world use. Comparing tulisokibart (26.5%, composite) vs. duvakitug (48%, composite) requires confirming both trials defined composite remission identically — a 48% composite result is not the same as a 48% treatment-policy result.
Patient selection: Biologic-naive vs. biologic-experienced patients respond very differently to anti-TL1A agents. Companion diagnostics (TNFSF15 haplotype) can enrich for responders, but a more restrictive label limits commercial opportunity.

Estimand Intelligence — Why the Same Endpoint Can Mean Different Things
ICH E9(R1) · Critical for interpreting IBD remission rates across TL1A trials
Essential BD Context ▼
An estimand defines the exact clinical question a trial is trying to answer — not just the endpoint, but how the trial handles real-world disruptions like rescue medication use, dose changes, discontinuations, and dropouts. Two IBD trials can both report "Week 52 remission" while answering fundamentally different questions. ICH E9(R1) (2019) requires sponsors to pre-specify the estimand before the trial starts.
Composite
Most common in IBD. Intercurrent events (rescue steroids, prohibited meds, discontinuation) are incorporated into the outcome — patients who need rescue are counted as non-responders. Measures real-world treatment success including tolerability. Industry standard for UC/CD Phase 2b/3.
Treatment Policy
ITT regardless of intercurrent events. All patients analyzed based on original assignment, whatever happened. Measures the drug's effect in practice. Can inflate apparent remission rates if patients got rescue.
Hypothetical
Ideal efficacy if the intercurrent event hadn't occurred. Estimates what remission would look like if no patient needed rescue. Useful for mechanism understanding but rarely used as primary estimand in late-stage IBD.
While on Treatment
Outcome measured only while patient stays on therapy. Excludes post-discontinuation outcomes. Underestimates real-world effectiveness for chronic diseases with high discontinuation.
BD read-through for IBD: When comparing TL1A remission rates across tulisokibart (Merck), duvakitug (Teva/Sanofi), and afimkibart (Roche), verify that all three are using a composite estimand with the same intercurrent event definition. A composite-based 35% remission rate is not comparable to a treatment-policy-based 45% remission rate. Future TL1A × IL-23p19 Phase 2 trials should pre-specify composite estimands to align with the emerging IBD standard.

TSLP × IL-33 / ST2 Axis · Biology Deep Dive
What is TSLP?
TSLP (Thymic Stromal Lymphopoietin) is an IL-7-related cytokine secreted by epithelial cells in the airway, skin, and gut in response to environmental triggers — allergens, pollutants, viruses. It signals through the TSLP receptor complex (TSLPR + IL-7Rα), activating dendritic cells, mast cells, basophils, and ILC2s. TSLP sits at the apex of the type 2 inflammatory cascade — blocking it upstream of IL-4/IL-13/IL-5 is why tezepelumab works pan-population, regardless of eosinophil count or IgE levels.
IL-33 / ST2 Axis — The Second Alarmin
IL-33 is an IL-1 family member stored preformed in the nucleus, released upon cell damage by allergen proteases or viral infection. It signals through ST2 (IL-1RL1), expressed on mast cells, ILC2s, Th2 cells, and eosinophils. Unlike TSLP (which primarily activates dendritic cells), IL-33 has direct effector cell activation — making them non-redundant. Blocking TSLP allows IL-33-driven ILC2 activation to persist, and vice versa. This is the mechanistic rationale for TSLP×IL-33 dual blockade.
COPD Biology — Why Alarmins Matter Beyond Asthma
COPD was historically a neutrophilic, non-type-2 disease. However, ex-smokers with eosinophilic features respond to IL-33 blockade (itepekimab AERIFY, 2025). IL-33 levels spike during viral AECOPD events, amplifying inflammatory cascades. TSLP upregulation occurs in COPD airways, particularly in ex-smokers with residual type-2 features. The dual alarmin hypothesis in COPD: blocking both TSLP and IL-33 could suppress both initiation and amplification phases of AECOPD, providing broader and more durable protection than either alone.

IL-4Rα × TSLP Type 2 Inflammation · Biology Deep Dive
IL-4Rα Signaling — Why It Drives Type 2 Disease
IL-4Rα (IL-4 receptor alpha chain) is the shared receptor subunit for both IL-4 and IL-13 signaling. IL-4 signals through the type I receptor (IL-4Rα + γc chain) on lymphocytes, driving Th2 differentiation, IgE class switching, and GATA-3 expression. IL-13 signals through the type II receptor (IL-4Rα + IL-13Rα1) on epithelial cells, smooth muscle, and fibroblasts, driving mucus production, barrier dysfunction, and fibrosis. Dupilumab blocks both IL-4 and IL-13 by targeting IL-4Rα — which explains its breadth across AD, asthma, COPD, eosinophilic esophagitis, prurigo nodularis, CRSwNP, and alopecia areata.
TSLP Upstream of IL-4Rα Signaling
TSLP (Thymic Stromal Lymphopoietin) activates dendritic cells and ILC2s to produce IL-4, IL-5, IL-9, and IL-13 — the very cytokines downstream of IL-4Rα. Blocking TSLP alone eliminates the initiation signal; blocking IL-4Rα eliminates the effector response. Together, dual blockade theoretically prevents both initiation (TSLP) and downstream amplification (IL-4Rα), eliminating the most common escape route when only one is blocked.
Atopic March — The Multi-Indication Thesis
The atopic march describes the progression from atopic dermatitis in infancy → allergic rhinitis and food allergy in childhood → asthma in adolescence/adulthood. TSLP is elevated at every stage of this progression. IL-4Rα signaling drives each transition. A bispecific targeting both nodes could theoretically interrupt the march — preventing atopic progression in young patients — a much larger addressable market than treating established disease.

Co-primary with EASI in most AD pivotals. IGA 0/1 = clear or almost clear skin on 5-point scale. Dupilumab SOC: ~37–40% IGA 0/1 Week 16. Bispecific must show ≥40–45% to claim superiority.

IL-4Rα × OX40L — AD Biology · Biology Deep Dive
OX40/OX40L Pathway — Memory T Cell Driver
OX40 (CD134, TNFRSF4) is expressed on activated T cells; OX40L (CD252, TNFSF4) is expressed on APCs (dendritic cells, B cells, mast cells). The OX40-OX40L interaction is critical for memory T cell survival and differentiation — it provides the co-stimulatory "third signal" that determines whether activated T cells become effector memory cells (persisting for years) or undergo clonal deletion. In AD, chronically inflamed skin generates memory Th2 cells that re-activate upon minimal allergen re-exposure. Blocking OX40L prevents this re-sensitization — theoretically enabling deeper and more durable remission than cytokine blockade alone.
IL-4Rα in AD — The Effector Driver
IL-4Rα drives the acute effector phase of AD: IL-4 promotes Th2 differentiation and IgE class switching; IL-13 directly disrupts the epidermal barrier (downregulates filaggrin, loricrin) and drives TSLP/IL-25 release — creating a self-perpetuating Th2 loop. Dupilumab's IL-4Rα blockade breaks this loop acutely. However, it does not address the memory T cell pool that was established before treatment — which may explain why some patients relapse when dupilumab is discontinued.
The Dual Block Hypothesis in AD
Combining OX40L blockade (memory suppression) with IL-4Rα blockade (effector suppression) theoretically addresses both the initiation of flares (memory re-activation) and the execution of inflammation (IL-4/IL-13 effector cytokines). The hypothesis predicts: (1) deeper short-term remission than either alone; (2) longer remission duration upon drug discontinuation (because the memory T cell pool is also being depleted). This is not proven clinically yet — the ZW171 IND filing and any bispecific Phase 1 data will be the first test of this hypothesis in humans.

IGF1R × TSHR TED Biology · Biology Deep Dive
IGF1R in TED — The Orbital Fibroblast Driver
IGF-1R (Insulin-like Growth Factor 1 Receptor) is overexpressed on orbital fibroblasts in TED patients. Upon activation by IGF-1 (or by IGF-1R cross-activation from TSHR), orbital fibroblasts differentiate into myofibroblasts (causing fibrosis) or fibrocytes/hyalocytes that produce excessive hyaluronan and glycosaminoglycans — causing orbital tissue expansion and proptosis. Teprotumumab (anti-IGF-1R mAb) blocks this fibroblast activation, explaining its dramatic proptosis reduction in the OPTIC trial.
TSHR Cross-Talk — Why Bispecific?
TSHR (Thyroid Stimulating Hormone Receptor) is the autoimmune target in Graves' disease: anti-TSHR antibodies (TSAbs) stimulate TSHR on thyroid follicular cells (causing hyperthyroidism) and on orbital fibroblasts (causing TED). TSAbs acting on orbital fibroblast TSHR activate the IGF-1R signaling pathway through physical receptor cross-talk (TSHR and IGF-1R form a signaling complex on fibroblast membranes). Blocking TSHR removes the autoimmune stimulus; blocking IGF-1R prevents downstream fibroblast activation. A bispecific targeting both receptors eliminates both the trigger (TSHR) and the effector (IGF-1R) — a mechanistically complete approach.
Hearing Loss Risk — The Sensorineural Issue
Teprotumumab is associated with sensorineural hearing loss (SNHL) in ~10–30% of patients (FDA label warning). The mechanism: IGF-1R is expressed on cochlear hair cells, which depend on IGF-1 for maintenance. IGF-1R blockade may impair cochlear hair cell survival. A bispecific that reduces the required anti-IGF-1R dose (by synergizing with TSHR blockade) could reduce SNHL risk. This is a clinically meaningful differentiator — hearing loss is the single biggest commercial limitation of teprotumumab and a key BD talking point.

FcRn (Neonatal Fc Receptor) Biology · Biology Deep Dive
How FcRn Works — The IgG Recycling Mechanism
FcRn (Neonatal Fc Receptor, also encoded by FCGRT) is expressed on endosomal membranes inside vascular endothelial cells and macrophages. Its primary function: prevent lysosomal degradation of IgG antibodies. When IgG is taken up by pinocytosis from circulation, it binds FcRn in the acidic endosome (pH 6.0), escapes degradation, and is recycled back to the cell surface where it's released at physiologic pH (7.4). Blocking FcRn prevents this recycling — IgG that enters the endosome is degraded, reducing total IgG half-life from ~21 days to ~3–5 days and circulating IgG levels by 60–85%.
Why IgG Reduction Treats Autoimmune Disease
In IgG-mediated autoimmune diseases (gMG: anti-AChR antibodies destroy neuromuscular junction; CIDP: anti-NF155/CASPR1/2 antibodies disrupt peripheral nerve nodal architecture; pemphigus vulgaris: anti-desmoglein antibodies destroy skin epithelial adhesion), pathogenic IgG autoantibodies are the direct disease driver. Reducing total IgG by 60–85% dilutes the pathogenic autoantibody titer proportionally. This is why FcRn inhibitors work across multiple unrelated autoimmune diseases — the mechanism is agnostic to the specific autoantibody target.
FcRn Bispecific Rationale — What Does a Second Arm Add?
FcRn monoAbs achieve IgG reduction but don't suppress the B cell source of autoantibodies. After treatment, autoantibody titers rebound when FcRn inhibitor is withdrawn. A bispecific adding B cell depletion (anti-CD19) addresses both IgG clearance AND long-term autoantibody source suppression — potentially enabling drug holidays or less frequent maintenance dosing. Albumin sparing: FcRn also recycles albumin; anti-FcRn antibodies that co-block albumin binding cause hypoalbuminemia. The engineering challenge is designing bispecifics that maintain selective IgG recycling blockade without albumin binding disruption.

BCMA × CD19 × CD3 — B Cell Immune Reset · Biology Deep Dive
Why BCMA + CD19 + CD3 for Immune Reset?
CD19 is expressed on all mature B cells from pre-B cells through plasmablasts — including the B cells that are activated by autoantigens and produce pathogenic autoantibodies. Depleting CD19+ cells eliminates the "recent" autoantibody-producing population. BCMA (B Cell Maturation Antigen, TNFRSF17) is expressed on long-lived plasma cells in the bone marrow — the cells that were established years or decades ago and continuously produce autoantibodies even after CD19+ B cell depletion. Combining anti-BCMA (plasma cell depletion) with anti-CD19 (mature B cell depletion) addresses both the existing autoantibody reservoir and the ongoing B cell activation. CD3 engagement recruits cytotoxic T cells (CTLs) to kill the B cell/plasma cell targets — the same mechanism as bispecific T cell engagers (BiTEs) in oncology, now applied to autoimmune.
Why CAR-T Works — And What Antibodies May Match
CD19 CAR-T in refractory SLE achieves 90–99% peripheral B cell depletion within 1–2 weeks, followed by bone marrow plasma cell depletion. The Erlangen group showed 5/5 refractory SLE patients achieved drug-free remission at 2 years after CD19 CAR-T alone. The reconstituted B cell repertoire appeared "naïve" — re-educated without autoimmune memory. An antibody-based trispecific (BCMA×CD19×CD3) attempting to replicate this must achieve comparable depletion depth. The advantage of antibody: no lymphodepletion required, outpatient administration, scalable manufacturing. The risk: antibody may not achieve the same depletion depth as CAR-T; cytokine release syndrome (CRS) risk must be managed.
Disease Biology — SLE as the Lead Indication
SLE (Systemic Lupus Erythematosus) is characterized by pathogenic IgG autoantibodies targeting nuclear antigens (anti-dsDNA, anti-Sm, anti-Ro/La). These drive complement activation, immune complex deposition, tissue inflammation, and end-organ damage (particularly lupus nephritis — LN). Both mature B cells (CD19+) and long-lived plasma cells (BCMA+) contribute to the persistent autoantibody load. The type I IFN pathway (targeted by anifrolumab) amplifies B cell autoreactivity. A complete immune reset targeting B cells + plasma cells + T cell-mediated killing would address the root cause of SLE in a way that no existing therapy achieves.

Direct: same target + same patients — head-to-head competition. Adjacent: related mechanism or nearby indication — compared by physicians or in trials. Same-Space: same broad disease area, different enough not to be a direct threat. The most important label in Meridian for BD work.
Enrichment
The automated process that takes a company name + area, gives Claude structured context, and uses its output to fill in drugs, trials, catalysts, and deals. "Enriching UCB" = running the pipeline on UCB for TL1A.
Completeness Score (0–100)
How thoroughly a company/drug has been researched — not how important it is. 0 = not enriched yet. 70+ = strong coverage across all 6 research stages.
Canonical Drug / Canonical ID
One master record per real molecule regardless of how many names it has. "Omvontis" = "RVT-3101" = tulisokibart → one canonical ID. All trials and deals link to that ID, not the name.
Catalyst
A future event that matters for BD — trial readout, FDA filing, conference presentation. Stored with a date. Drives the catalyst timeline on the home page.
Discovery Queue
New companies or drugs Meridian found but hasn't fully researched. Meridian's to-do list for new entities.
drug_area_scores
The database table where Claude's competitive assessments live. One row per drug per area. Stores overlap classification, rationale, vs_ailux positioning. This is the output of enrichment, the input to the dashboard.
Confidence Level — confirmed / supported / inferred
Confirmed: Claude found a direct source. Supported: multiple indirect sources agree. Inferred: Claude reasoned from related evidence. Unverified: claimed but not corroborated. Stored in every record — not yet shown in the UI.
Prompt / Prompt Engineering
The exact instruction text given to Claude. Every classification, summary, and score in Meridian is the output of a specific prompt. Changing the prompt changes all future outputs. The prompts are the most important text in the system.
Supabase
The cloud database where all Meridian data lives. The dashboard reads from it in real time. The enrichment scripts write to it. Think: structured cloud spreadsheet with 20+ linked tables.
Knowledge Graph
Data organized as nodes (company, drug, trial, deal) connected by edges (CONTROLS, TARGETS, PARTNERED_WITH). Unlike a spreadsheet, a graph lets you traverse connections — "which companies that did TL1A deals also have Phase 2 trials?" becomes a traversal, not a manual lookup.
Migration
A versioned change to the database structure — adding a column, creating a new table. Meridian has 21+ migrations applied in order (v1–v21). Each represents a structural capability upgrade.

The diagram above also shows a correction to how Meridian originally organized biology. The old disease_areas table listed targets (TL1A, TSLP, FcRn) as if they were the top-level category. Because facts are stored separately and connected by pointers, one fact update fixes the display everywhere at once. If UCB renames a drug, you change one row in drugs and it propagates to every tab, every card, every filter result. And because targets are now normalized rows (not free text), Meridian can answer questions like "which disease areas share a target?" or "what modality is most common in IBD?" — questions that were structurally impossible before.

🔬 A Drug Gets Classified — Full Pipeline Trace ▾
Here is the complete, unabridged sequence of what happens when the enrichment pipeline classifies cizutamig (UCB) as a Direct TL1A competitor. Every step, every query, every piece of data that moves.
1
Pipeline starts — reads the work queue from Supabase
The pipeline reads from tables companies, company_areas, and disease_areas — asking: which companies are tagged to this area and haven't been enriched recently? Companies with status = acquired are excluded (they've been absorbed into another company). The result is sorted so the least-recently-enriched company goes first.
This returns: "UCB, area: TL1A — enriched 3 days ago." UCB moves to the front of the queue because it hasn't been enriched recently.
2
Pipeline fetches everything Meridian already knows about UCB
The pipeline reads everything Meridian already knows about UCB — from tables drugs, drug_area_scores, and catalysts. This existing knowledge gets included in what Claude receives, so Claude builds on prior analysis rather than starting from scratch each time.
This builds the context object — a structured summary of what Meridian already knows. This context gets included in the prompt so Claude doesn't start from scratch.
3
Pipeline calls Claude — Step 3: Drug Discovery
Claude receives a brief about UCB: the company name, disease area focus (TL1A / IBD), which drugs are already in the database, and recent catalysts. Claude is asked to identify all drugs in UCB's TL1A pipeline — name, mechanism, target, clinical stage, and indication. It also assigns a confidence_level to each drug: confirmed if it found a direct source, supported if indirect sources agree, or inferred if it reasoned from available evidence.
Claude returns structured JSON. The pipeline validates it, checks canonical identity (is "cizutamig" already in the database?), then writes each drug as a row in drugs.
4
Pipeline calls Claude again — Step 5: Competitive Classification
Claude receives the drug facts for cizutamig (mechanism: anti-TL1A antibody, phase: Phase 2, indication: Crohn's and UC, company: UCB) along with the full four-tier classification definitions from the ailux_positions table — the exact Direct / Adjacent / Same-Space / Watch criteria shown in the "Definitions" section above. Claude applies those definitions and returns: which tier this drug belongs to, a one-sentence rationale explaining why, a direct comparison to SPY002, and a relevance score from 1–10.
This is the classification call. The exact wording of Direct/Adjacent/Same-Space definitions determines what Claude decides. These words are the most important text in the system.
5
Claude returns a structured conclusion
{
  "overlap": "Direct",
  "overlap_rationale": "cizutamig is an anti-TL1A mAb in Phase 2 for Crohn's and UC — the same target and same patient population as Ailux's bispecific.",
  "vs_ailux_positioning": "Head-to-head competitor. UCB's monospecific vs. Ailux's bispecific will be compared on depth of remission and combo potential.",
  "area_fit": 9,
  "area_fit_rationale": "Core TL1A IBD program — directly relevant to Ailux's space."
}
6
Pipeline writes the conclusion back to Supabase
Claude's output is written into the drug_area_scores table — one row per drug per area. If a score already exists for this drug in this area, it gets overwritten with the new assessment. Fields stored include overlap (the tier), overlap_rationale (the reasoning), vs_ailux (the comparison), and area_fit (a relevance score from 1–10).
The ON CONFLICT ... DO UPDATE part means: if this drug already has a score for this area, overwrite it. So re-running enrichment updates existing records rather than creating duplicates.
7
Dashboard reads it — and assembles the display
When you open the TL1A tab, the dashboard reads from drug_area_scores, joins it with drugs and companies to get the full picture, filters to area TL1A, and sorts by relevance score — highest first. The "Direct" badge, the rationale text, the comparison note — all come from those stored fields. Nothing is calculated live; it all comes from what Claude wrote during enrichment.
The browser reads this table join and renders the TL1A drug cards. The "Direct" badge, the rationale text, the vs_ailux note — all assembled from those six columns. The HTML you see is built from these rows in real time on every page load.
💡 The Implication
If you change the definition of "Direct" in Step 4's prompt — even one sentence — every future enrichment run will apply the new definition. The old stored scores in Supabase stay as they were. A re-enrichment is needed to update them. This is why the ground truth validation framework exists: you run it after every prompt change to verify the new definition produces the right answers before deploying.

📐 The Definitions That Drive Every Classification ▾
Every drug in Meridian gets classified into one of four tiers. These are the exact definitions Claude reads before making that decision. They live in a database table called ailux_positions — one row per disease area. Changing these definitions changes every future classification. The text below is pulled directly from that table for the IBD / TL1A area.
Ailux's Drug — The Reference Point for Every Classification
Drug: SPY002  ·  Targets: TL1A × IL-23p19  ·  Format: bispecific antibody, subcutaneous  ·  Stage: Phase 2 (UC and CD)
Spyre Therapeutics is developing SPY002, a bispecific antibody that blocks both TL1A (which drives mucosal inflammation in IBD) and IL-23p19 (which drives the Th17 immune pathway). The idea is that blocking both targets in a single injection is more effective than blocking either alone — and more convenient than taking two separate drugs. Ailux's BD strategy is to track and partner around Spyre's competitive position.

Direct Field name stored in database: overlap = "Direct"
What it means: The drug targets TL1A — the same primary target as Ailux — or is a combination program that includes TL1A as one of its components. This is a direct mechanistic threat regardless of what stage the drug is at. Even a preclinical TL1A program counts as Direct.
Real examples: ABBV-701 (AbbVie's anti-TL1A antibody), tulisokibart / MK-7240 (Merck's anti-TL1A), afimkibart / RO7121 (Roche anti-TL1A), duvakitug / SAR447029 (Sanofi anti-TL1A), SIM0709 (BI / Simcere anti-TL1A), and any TL1A × IL-23 bispecific from a competitor company.
Suggest a change to the Direct definition
Adjacent Field name stored in database: overlap = "Adjacent"
What it means: The drug treats IBD (UC or Crohn's) but works through a different mechanism than TL1A. These drugs matter because they either validate the same biology Ailux is working in, or they're being tested as combination partners with TL1A inhibitors. They're not head-to-head competitors — but they shape the treatment landscape that Ailux's drug will enter.
Real examples: Skyrizi / risankizumab (AbbVie's IL-23 blocker, approved for UC and Crohn's), ABBV-382 (AbbVie's α4β7 integrin blocker), vedolizumab / Entyvio (Takeda's α4β7 blocker), mirikizumab / Omvoh (Lilly's IL-23 blocker), ABBV-466 (AbbVie's IL-23 + α4β7 combination program), and any bispecific combining IL-23 with another gut-targeted mechanism.
Suggest a change to the Adjacent definition
Same-Space Field name stored in database: overlap = "Same-Space"
What it means: Approved standard-of-care drugs for IBD that work through a completely different pathway — not TL1A, not IL-23, not α4β7. These drugs treat the same patients Ailux's drug will treat, so they define the efficacy bar that Ailux needs to beat. They're not a mechanistic threat, but they are the commercial benchmark.
Real examples: Ustekinumab / Stelara (J&J's IL-12/23p40 blocker — approved for UC and Crohn's; uses the older, broader p40 target rather than the more selective IL-23p19).
Suggest a change to the Same-Space definition
Watch Field name stored in database: overlap = "Watch"
What it means: IBD drugs with entirely different mechanisms — JAK inhibitors, S1P modulators, TNF inhibitors, RIPK1 inhibitors, and others. These compete for the same patients but through biology unrelated to TL1A or its neighbors. Meridian monitors them for patient-share pressure, safety signals that could affect the whole class, and any signs they're being tested in combination with TL1A drugs.
Real examples: Rinvoq / upadacitinib (AbbVie JAK1), ozanimod (BMS S1P), etrasimod (Pfizer S1P), filgotinib (Galapagos JAK), tofacitinib (Pfizer JAK), TNF inhibitors including Humira, Remicade, and Simponi, lutikizumab (AbbVie IL-1α/β), ABBV-668 (AbbVie RIPK1).

What Gets Written About Each Company
For every company in every disease area, Claude writes five specific assessments. These are stored in the database under the company's profile and shown on the dashboard cards. Here is what each one is supposed to capture:
vs_ailux — Mechanism comparison
One or two sentences directly comparing this company's drug to SPY002 — mechanism difference, stage difference, and what that means for competition. For companies not in Ailux's primary areas, this explains why Ailux watches them instead (benchmark value, potential acquirer, cross-area signal). Claude is instructed never to write "no overlap" — there is always a BD reason to monitor.
why_it_matters — BD reason
One or two sentences answering: why does this company matter to Ailux's BD strategy specifically? The answer must be one of: they set a pricing benchmark Ailux should track, they are a potential partner or acquirer, their clinical data validates Ailux's mechanism, or their deal structure defines what counterparties expect. Generic answers are rejected by the prompt.
key_risk — What could go wrong
One or two sentences on the single most important risk for this company's program — trial risk (endpoint design, enrollment problems, regulatory uncertainty), competitive risk (a head-to-head trial coming, a faster competitor), platform risk (the technology itself), or financial risk. Claude is instructed to be specific — not generic summaries.
bd_intelligence.profile — BD behavior classification
Claude classifies each company's dominant BD behavior as one of five types: acquirer (they buy things), licensor (they sell or out-license programs), collaborator (they prefer co-development), partner-friendly (open to deals, history shows it), or internal-focused (they build and keep everything in-house).
platform_intelligence.assessment — Strategic conclusion
A single sentence answering: what does this company's platform trajectory mean for Ailux's competitive positioning or BD timing? For companies in Ailux's primary disease area, this is about competitive threat and timing. For companies in other areas Ailux monitors (FcRn, T-cell, etc.), this is about benchmark value, partnership potential, or cross-area intelligence.

🎛️ How Scoring Works — And How Changing the Prompt Changes the Score ▾
Meridian uses three scoring systems. Each one is an instruction to Claude — not a formula that runs on numbers. Understanding what each prompt asks for makes the scores interpretable. And knowing that the prompt is editable makes the scores improvable.
Score 1: Overlap Classification (Direct / Adjacent / Same-Space)
What the prompt asks
"Given this drug's mechanism and indication, how directly does it compete with Ailux's TL1A bispecific? Use: Direct = same target + same patients, Adjacent = related overlap, Same-Space = shared disease area only."
How to improve accuracy
If a drug is mis-classified, the fix is almost always a clarification to the definition. Example: adding "bispecifics with TL1A as one arm count as Direct" would change how bispecifics are classified without re-running any data pulls.
Score 2: Completeness Score (0–100)
What the scoring checks
Stage 1 — Entity Discovery (10pts): Does this company have a profile entry at all?
Stage 2 — Drug Mapping (15pts): Are drugs linked to this company?
Stage 3 — Trial Intelligence (20pts): Are there ClinicalTrials.gov records for those drugs?
Stage 4 — Catalyst Engine (15pts): Are there upcoming readout dates?
Stage 5 — Strategic Positioning (25pts): Is vs_ailux_positioning written? This is weighted highest because it is the most valuable for BD.
Stage 6 — Deal Intelligence (15pts): Are there deal records?
Key nuance
A score of 0 does not mean bad data — it means the scoring script hasn't processed that entity yet. This was the exact bug found in May 2026: 6 companies showed score=0 but had rich data. The scoring script had simply never been pointed at them. Always check last_scored_at before acting on a low score.

If Stage 5 (Strategic Positioning) matters most to you, you could reweight it from 25pts to 40pts. That would reprioritize the research queue to surface companies missing vs_ailux analysis first.
Score 3: Area Fit (1–10)
What the prompt asks
"On a scale of 1–10, how relevant is this drug to Ailux's TL1A IBD program? 10 = core direct competitor that Ailux must track closely. 1 = only tangentially related to the area."
How it's used
Area fit drives the sort order in the area tabs — drugs with area_fit=9 show first. If you want to see a broader competitive landscape (including drugs you wouldn't normally prioritize), lowering the filter threshold from 7 to 5 would surface more entries. This is a one-line code change in the dashboard.
💡 The Feedback Loop
The ground truth validation framework (28 TL1A test cases) is exactly this loop: change prompt definition → re-run enrichment on test cases → check if known-correct answers still pass → if yes, deploy. Before this framework existed, there was no way to know if a prompt change made things better or worse. Now every change is measurable.

🕸️ Questions Meridian Can't Answer Yet ▾
These are strategic BD questions that the current data model cannot compute directly — not because the information doesn't exist, but because the right connections between records haven't been built yet. Each one is one missing relationship away from being answerable.
"Which drugs directly compete with Ailux's TL1A bispecific?" Blocked
Overlap classification exists per drug, but there's no drug → COMPETES_WITH → drug edge. You can see that two drugs are both "Direct" competitors, but the system can't directly link them. Fix: one migration + backfill from existing drug_area_scores. Highest leverage, lowest effort.
"Which mechanism class is most crowded for TL1A?" Blocked
Drug targets are stored as free text ("TL1A", "BCMA×CD3"). "Which drugs share TL1A as target" requires a text search, not a database join. Fix: a targets table where each target is a proper node with a drug→TARGETS→target relationship.
"Which deal brought this drug into this company's portfolio?" Partially blocked
