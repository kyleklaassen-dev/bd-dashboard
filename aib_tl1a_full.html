2 · The scientific thesis — reasoning from mechanism to conclusion

This section builds the argument step by step, because the investment case is a scientific case.

Step 1 — Target engagement is necessary but not sufficient

Anti-TL1A antibodies achieve near-complete target engagement: tulisokibart suppresses free soluble TL1A by ~90%, afimkibart by ~92%. Yet clinical remission lands at only ~26-28%. Engagement is essentially maximal; efficacy is not. The drug is doing its molecular job — and the disease continues.56

Step 2 — Why the gap exists (the escape pathway)

Meridian's pharmacodynamic analysis attributes the disconnect to IL-23-independent Th17 activation and epithelial-restitution failure that TL1A blockade does not touch. Independently, the non-responder biology agrees: ~72% of anti-TL1A non-responders escape through Th17 routes; ~55% of anti-IL-23 non-responders escape through Th1/IL-12-independent IFN-γ. The two escape maps barely overlap — which is the mechanistic definition of a combination opportunity.78

Step 3 — Why IL-23p19 is the correct second arm

IL-23p19 blockade addresses Th17 persistence and maintenance biology — the exact route TL1A leaves open — while TL1A covers the inflammation and fibro-stromal remodeling that IL-23 agents miss. Selecting p19 (not p40) preserves IL-12/Th1 immunity and matches the validated risankizumab/mirikizumab class, avoiding the redundancy and safety baggage of p40.9

Step 4 — The testable conclusion

Synthesis. If the target-engagement-to-remission gap is caused by a non-overlapping IL-23-dependent escape pathway, then closing both pathways with one molecule should convert engagement into remission — the basis for the 30-40% deep-remission hypothesis. This is falsifiable: if dual blockade does not beat the best monospecific (RO7837195 and MT-251 are the first reads), the thesis fails. That is the single most important experiment in this area, and it has not yet reported.

4 · Molecular design, manufacturing & biomarkers (for the CSO)

At the protein-engineering level, three design axes separate winners from also-rans in this class:

  • Half-life engineering. XmAb942 (~74 d) shows Fc/FcRn optimization can roughly triple a native antibody's half-life. MT-251 (Mirador) is explicitly built on pH-sensitive antigen binding + FcRn recycling for quarterly-plus dosing. A bispecific that does not match this will lose on convenience even if efficacy ties.

  • Format & valency. Approaches range from IgG-like dual-targeting antibodies to single-domain (VHH) constructs (e.g., LQ080's extended-half-life sdAb) to fixed-dose combinations (Spyre's SPY230 = two separate mAbs). Single-molecule bispecifics win on CMC simplicity and payer story; combinations win on speed but carry two-asset cost and complexity.

  • Arm selection (p19 vs p40). p19 selectivity preserves IL-12/Th1 immunity and inherits the risankizumab safety/efficacy precedent; p40 (RO7837195) blocks IL-12 too — broader, but with the redundancy and historical safety questions of the ustekinumab era.

ALX001 design scorecard (what 'best-in-class' requires here): (1) p19-selective; (2) Fc-engineered for ~Q12W SC; (3) balanced potency on both arms to deliver additive — not merely additive-on-paper — efficacy; (4) a manufacturable single-molecule format. The competitive set shows each trait exists separately; none has yet combined all four with clinical proof.

The manufacturing tax (CMC)

Single-molecule bispecifics carry very-high CMC complexity — chain mispairing, aggregation, lower yields, and harder comparability — versus high for a monospecific mAb. This is a real cost and timeline burden, and it is also a barrier to entry: it is part of why the bispecific clinic is thin despite obvious interest. A combination (e.g., SPY230's two mAbs) sidesteps the engineering risk but doubles cost of goods and complicates the payer story.24

Biomarker & precision-medicine strategy

Three biomarker tiers operate in this area: predictive/companion-diagnostic (HLA-DQA1*05; the TL1A genetic-response signature pioneered by tulisokibart's program); non-responder / escape (OSM / the OSM-OSMR axis); and PD-monitoring (soluble TL1A, fecal calprotectin, CRP).25

The sharpest strategic insight in this brief. OSM is the escape-pathway marker that flags the patients who FAIL anti-TL1A monotherapy — and it is exactly the population dual blockade should rescue. Meridian's data show ALX001's anti-TL1A arm already tracks OSM and fecal calprotectin. If tulisokibart wins the CDx-positive responder segment, ALX001 can position deliberately for the OSM-high / CDx-negative escape population — turning a competitor's precision strategy into ALX001's addressable beachhead.

5 · Regulatory, safety & immunogenicity

A pioneer arm and an established arm. Anti-IL-23p19 is fully precedented — three approvals since 2017, and risankizumab's PsO→PsA→CD→UC trajectory is the canonical multi-indication playbook, so the dermatology-to-IBD path is mapped. Anti-TL1A is a pioneer mechanism with zero approvals; crucially, the IBD endpoints themselves are precedented (modified Mayo, SES-CD, the emerging deep-remission composite), so the regulatory risk is not endpoint acceptance — it is demonstrating superiority/non-inferiority against an increasingly good standard of care.26

Implication for ALX001: the bispecific inherits a de-risked endpoint framework and half its mechanism's regulatory precedent. The bar it must clear is clinical, not regulatory — additive efficacy on accepted endpoints.

Safety & immunogenicity — the other half of the bet

Efficacy is only half the case; for dual immunosuppression, safety is the other half. The encouraging backdrop: the anti-IL-23p19 class has a notably clean infection and malignancy profile (risankizumab), so the IL-23 arm adds efficacy without the safety cost that anti-TNF carries. Anti-TL1A has shown no class-halting safety signal to date — Merck has presented 50-week infection-AE data for tulisokibart (ECCO 2025 P0749, DDW 2025 Tu1933) consistent with the class.27

The open question for a bispecific: does simultaneous TL1A + IL-23 blockade raise infection risk beyond either pathway alone? Mechanistically it need not — the two pathways are partly non-overlapping and IL-23 blockade is well-tolerated — but it is unproven either way, and it is the safety endpoint a partner's diligence will scrutinize first. Confidence: the favorable IL-23 backdrop is HIGH; the additive-safety question is LOW until bispecific data read out.

Would gastroenterologists switch? (the prescriber test)

'Can it work?' is the science question; 'would doctors switch?' is the commercial one — and they have different answers [J]. A profile of ~38% remission, clean safety, Q12W SC would likely win new-start and post-IL-23 patients on convenience and the dual-mechanism story, but would not by itself displace an entrenched Skyrizi or a CDx-enriched tulisokibart in stable patients. To drive a switch — and the $10B-tier deal — ALX001 most likely needs at least one of: fistula superiority, an anti-fibrotic/transmural-healing benefit, or a clear deep-remission advantage. Those are different commercial futures, and they should shape the Phase 2 endpoint strategy now.

China dynamics & licensing-in optionality

A defining feature of this area that Western-centric analyses miss: the bispecific lane is disproportionately China-origin — LaNova's LQ080 (licensed ex-China to Zymeworks), Simcere's SIM0709 (to Boehringer), plus NovaMab, Elpiscience (ES302), Biocad (BCD-261) and Qyuns (QX030N). China is moving fast and cheap on exactly this format.37

Why this is an Ailux advantage, not just a threat: Ailux is itself a Shanghai-based biotech, inside the same innovation hub — with the cost structure, speed, and local capital that hub confers. The BI–Simcere precedent shows the dominant deal pattern here is a Western pharma licensing-in a China-originated bispecific. That is plausibly ALX001's most likely path to a premium deal — and it argues for building Western-quality data packages and IP early to be the China asset a major chooses.

13 · What would change our view

Thesis: a TL1A×IL-23p19 bispecific can outperform monospecifics in IBD.

  • Tulisokibart ATLAS-UC remission exceeds expectations (bar a second mechanism can't economically clear).
