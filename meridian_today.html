Elpiscience's ECCO 2026 ES302 data (DOP122) closed the most significant intelligence gap in the competitive landscape: the second target is confirmed as IL-23p19, making ES302 a direct 2+2 symmetric bispecific competitor to ALX001 with preclinical proof-of-concept in DSS colitis and NHP PK supporting extended half-life. The Phase 3 ATLAS-UC primary readout, expected approximately November 2026, will either validate or invalidate that ceiling at scale. IL-23p19: The Approved Benchmark Is Now the Competitive Floor, Not the Ceiling

The IL-23p19 class is fully validated in IBD — risankizumab (AbbVie, approved UC and CD, $12B+ peak sales), mirikizumab (Lilly, approved UC), and guselkumab (J&J, CD Phase 3) define the efficacy floor against which any new IL-23p19-targeting program is benchmarked. The clinical question for ALX001 is not whether IL-23p19 blockade works in IBD — that is established — but what simultaneous TL1A engagement adds in patients who either failed IL-23 monotherapy or who carry the high-TNFSF15-expression genotype enriched among TL1A-responsive patients. AbbVie's ongoing TARGET-CD combination study (TL1A + Skyrizi in CD, H2 2026 data) will directly address the sequential-versus-simultaneous question for the bispecific thesis. That data, combined with tulisokibart Phase 3, will constitute the mechanistic brief that ALX001's Phase 2 design must be built around.

The Pfizer–Innovent collaboration — $10.5B total potential value, 12 programs spanning ADCs and multi-specific antibodies, announced May 29 — reinforces that Pfizer is building its multispecific execution infrastructure with deliberate intent. Pfizer's co-development of RO7837195, the IL-23p40×TL1A bispecific with Roche, will benefit from this expanded capability. The p40-versus-p19 subunit distinction matters mechanistically: IL-23p40 is shared with IL-12, meaning p40-targeted agents partially suppress IL-12-driven Th1 immunity in ways that p19-selective blockade does not. ALX001's selective IL-23p19 engagement preserves the IL-12/Th1 axis — a positioning argument that has clinical relevance for infectious disease surveillance, particularly relevant in the context of the obefazimod cancer safety signal that reminded the IBD community oncology surveillance is not optional.

AbbVie remains timing-constrained by the ABBV-701 Phase 1 readout (October 2026), but J&J is not — and its guselkumab-golimumab combination data have already demonstrated willingness to pursue dual-mechanism IBD strategies.

UC Small Molecule Safety: Obefazimod's Cancer Signal Redirects Prescriber Attention

Abivax's obefazimod is an oral RNA splicing modulator that reduces RORγt expression in ILC3s and effector T cells via a distinct mechanism from either TL1A blockade or IL-23 inhibition — it does not target either cytokine pathway directly but modulates the transcriptional landscape upstream. In the ~900,000 US UC patients (market $8.0B, 25% SOC remission rate, 40% biologic failure, unmet need 8/10), the drug demonstrated significant induction and maintenance efficacy, making the cancer safety signal that followed — severe enough to trigger a sharp equity selloff — particularly disruptive. Prescribers were confronted with the familiar small-molecule trade: efficacy at the cost of systemic immunomodulation with potentially oncogenic downstream effects. For a patient who has already failed biologics and faces progressive mucosal disease, the choice between efficacy-plus-malignancy-risk and a precision biologic mechanism is not abstract — it determines whether they stay on label or switch classes.

The obefazimod episode is a net positive for the TL1A bispecific class and for ALX001's partnering narrative in two ways. First, it reinforces that UC payers and prescribers are being reminded, again, that small-molecule immunomodulation in the gut carries systemic exposure risk that target-selective biologics do not. Second, it creates a prospective regulatory expectation: any new IBD program — including ALX001's Phase 2 — will face enhanced scrutiny for oncology surveillance endpoints. Building that into the Phase 2 design now, before the first patient is dosed, is operationally straightforward and positions ALX001's safety profile as a differentiator rather than a liability.

BD Lens

The obefazimod cancer signal will sharpen payer formulary positioning in UC toward biologic precision mechanisms over 2026–2027, particularly for patients with prior malignancy or high-risk profiles. ALX001's Phase 2 protocol should incorporate prospective oncology surveillance endpoints — not as a concession to regulatory pressure but as a proactive differentiator that lets Ailux make a clean safety comparison against both small molecules and the broader biologic class when entering Phase 2b BD discussions.

Ulcerative Colitis

There are approximately 900,000 patients with ulcerative colitis in the United States. The addressable market is estimated at $8.0B. Current standard-of-care achieves remission in approximately 25% of patients; 40% fail biologics, leaving a substantial refractory population. Unmet need score: 8/10. For the patient who has cycled through mesalamine, corticosteroids, and a vedolizumab or anti-TNF course, the therapeutic options narrow sharply, mucosal inflammation progresses, and quality of life deteriorates around unpredictable flares. A drug that achieves durable remission in that biologic-failure population — particularly via a mechanism that doesn't carry the systemic oncology risk that obefazimod's safety signal has now foregrounded — commands both clinical adoption and premium pricing.

BD Lens

The UC biologic failure rate (40%) defines ALX001's primary clinical hypothesis: the 360,000 US UC patients who do not respond to or lose response on existing biologics are the target population for a TL1A×IL-23p19 bispecific, and the 25% remission rate under SOC defines the clinical bar that Phase 2 must visibly exceed. The obefazimod cancer signal further concentrates prescriber attention on biologic precision, increasing the commercial premium for a mechanism-clean IBD program entering Phase 2 with robust oncology surveillance built in.

Crohn's Disease

There are approximately 780,000 patients with Crohn's disease in the United States. The addressable market is estimated at $7.0B. Current standard-of-care achieves remission in approximately 20% of patients; 45% fail biologics — the highest biologic failure rate among the major IBD indications — leaving a refractory population of roughly 350,000 patients with limited therapeutic options. Unmet need score: 9/10. CD's transmural inflammation, skip lesions, and fistulizing complications create a disease phenotype where partial mucosal response is clinically insufficient; patients require deep remission including endoscopic healing to prevent surgery and disability progression. The tulisokibart APOLLO-CD data — 49.1% clinical remission in 55 patients — represent the highest TL1A monotherapy remission rate disclosed in CD and set the benchmark that a bispecific must exceed to justify the format premium.

BD Lens

CD's 45% biologic failure rate and 9/10 unmet need score make it the highest-value indication for ALX001's bispecific hypothesis. A Phase 2 that enrolls biologic-failure CD patients and demonstrates endoscopic remission — not merely clinical response — at a rate exceeding the tulisokibart monospecific benchmark will constitute the strongest possible BD inflection event for ALX001. CD-first Phase 2 enrollment, rather than UC-first, should be on the table as a partnering narrative accelerant given the CD unmet need and the comparatively higher remission threshold that creates visible bispecific differentiation.

TL1A Target Area (Broad)

There are approximately 2,500,000 patients in the US addressable by TL1A-targeting biologics across IBD, SSc-ILD, HS, RA, and axial spondyloarthritis. The addressable market is estimated at $15.0B. Current standard-of-care achieves remission in approximately 18% of patients across this broad population; 42% fail biologics, representing the largest aggregate unmet need across any single cytokine target in the Ailux competitive landscape. Unmet need score: 9/10. The breadth of TL1A indication coverage — from IBD to ILD to skin and joint manifestations — is structurally underappreciated in the bispecific format, where adding IL-23p19 as the second target narrows the addressable population to IBD and psoriasis but raises the per-patient efficacy ceiling. ALX001's indication strategy will determine whether it captures the IBD-plus-dermatology opportunity or concentrates on the highest-unmet-need IBD population alone.

BD Lens

The $15.0B TL1A addressable market is not fully capturable by a bispecific IBD program alone — Ailux should build a Phase 2 indication strategy that explicitly sequences UC and CD as lead indications with psoriasis as a secondary label expansion target, consistent with the broad indication approach taken by Boan Biotech (BA2201, IBD + dermatology). The dermatology expansion angle strengthens the BD narrative for partners whose existing commercial infrastructure spans gastroenterology and dermatology — AbbVie (Skyrizi across IBD and psoriasis) being the canonical example once the ABBV-701 timing constraint clears.
