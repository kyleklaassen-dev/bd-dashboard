What does an institution look like when the engine is running but no one is steering?

Last week answered that question with unusual clarity. Between May 12 and May 15, the FDA approved three major drugs across three therapeutic areas — atezolizumab in ctDNA-guided bladder cancer, ENHERTU in early-stage HER2-positive breast cancer, and Fasenra in hypereosinophilic syndrome — while simultaneously losing its commissioner. Marty Makary resigned on the 12th. Kyle Diamantas, whose expertise is food regulation, stepped in as acting head of the agency responsible for 80% of the US drug supply. Three days later, the approval machine produced one of its most consequential weeks in memory.

The paradox is worth sitting with, because it reveals something structural about how the FDA actually works. The review divisions — CDER, CBER — have institutional momentum that outlasts any single commissioner. PDUFA deadlines don't pause for personnel changes. Advisory committees don't reschedule because the front office is in transition. The science keeps moving because the scientists keep working. But leadership vacuums have consequences that emerge on a longer timescale: IND guidance letters slow down, policy signals become ambiguous, and companies with novel mechanisms — the exact programs that need regulatory clarity most — start hedging their filing timelines.

This morning's briefing leads with the biggest of those three approvals: ENHERTU's double indication in early-stage HER2-positive breast cancer, which places an antibody-drug conjugate at both ends of the perioperative treatment arc for the first time. Then we'll look at the leadership question itself through a corporate strategy lens. Fasenra's HES approval rounds out the regulatory picture. And with ASCO abstract releases landing Thursday, we'll close with a preview of the most data-rich two weeks of 2026.

The tea is ready. Let's get into it.

ENHERTU Claims Both Ends of the Perioperative Arc

On May 15, the FDA approved fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan-nxki (ENHERTU, AstraZeneca / Daiichi Sankyo) for two new indications in adults with HER2-positive early-stage breast cancer. The first is neoadjuvant, based on DESTINY-Breast11. The second is adjuvant, based on DESTINY-Breast05. Together, they position a single ADC across the entire perioperative treatment pathway — before surgery and after it — displacing both anthracycline-based chemotherapy and trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1, Kadcyla, Roche) in the process.

The Neoadjuvant Shift: DESTINY-Breast11

DESTINY-Breast11 was a randomized, three-arm, open-label trial enrolling 927 patients with Stage II or III HER2-positive (IHC 3+ or ISH+) breast cancer. The key comparison: four cycles of T-DXd (5.4 mg/kg Q3W) followed by THP, versus dose-dense doxorubicin/cyclophosphamide (ddAC) followed by THP. The primary endpoint was pathological complete response (pCR).

The headline isn't the delta — it's the composition of the neoadjuvant regimen. The T-DXd arm eliminated anthracyclines entirely from the pre-surgical phase. Doxorubicin carries cumulative cardiotoxicity risk; removing it without sacrificing pCR is the structural advance. The ADC's bystander effect — HER2-expressing cells internalize the payload, then release cytotoxic metabolites that kill neighboring HER2-low cells — is the mechanism enabling this substitution.

The Adjuvant Standard Falls: DESTINY-Breast05

DESTINY-Breast05 asked whether T-DXd outperforms T-DM1 (Kadcyla) as adjuvant treatment in patients with residual invasive disease. T-DM1 had been the standard since the KATHERINE trial in 2019. The result was not close.

A hazard ratio of 0.47 means a 53% reduction in risk of recurrence or death. Kadcyla isn't just displaced — its clinical rationale in this population is essentially over.

The Clinical Development Frame: Surrogate Endpoints and Head-to-Head Design

These two trials illustrate surrogate endpoints and head-to-head comparative designs. In DESTINY-Breast11, pCR served as a surrogate — measurable at surgery rather than years later. The ICH E9(R1) estimand framework would classify pCR as a composite variable estimand integrating tumor response and treatment strategy into a single binary measure. DESTINY-Breast05 used IDFS in a direct head-to-head against the existing standard — the gold standard of comparative effectiveness.

The Unanswered Question: Sequential T-DXd Toxicity

32.7% of patients fail pCR after neoadjuvant T-DXd. They would then receive adjuvant T-DXd — but DESTINY-Breast05 enrolled T-DXd-naive patients. What is the cumulative ILD risk when T-DXd is given twice in sequence? This is an unanswered pharmacovigilance question.

The Gatekeeper Without a Guard: FDA After Makary

On May 12, Marty Makary resigned as FDA Commissioner after 13 months. His replacement, Kyle Diamantas, comes from the food safety division and has no drug review background. The White House had reportedly signed off on replacing Makary, driven by frustration over vape authorization delays, CDER/CBER turnover, and the Moderna mRNA flu vaccine application refusal.

Porter's Gatekeeper: The Regulator as Structural Force

Porter's Five Forces requires an addendum for pharma: the FDA as a gatekeeper controlling market entry with absolute authority. The gatekeeper's institutional capacity has been structurally degraded — by DOGE-era staffing reductions, review division turnover, and an acting commissioner from food safety. The effect: threat of new entrants increases as regulatory unpredictability raises entry costs. For Ailux's 2027 IND filings, the operational layer (PDUFA deadlines) is intact, but the strategic layer (policy guidance, novel frameworks) is in freefall.

Fasenra Extends the Eosinophil Franchise into HES

On May 14, the FDA approved benralizumab (Fasenra, AstraZeneca) for HES — a 65% reduction in first HES flare risk via the NATRON Phase III trial. This completes a franchise arc: severe eosinophilic asthma, EGPA, and now HES. Taking a single antibody and systematically expanding it into every eosinophil-driven disease is the label extension playbook for AI-designed antibody platforms.

ENHERTU's double approval is the 发 (fā, "release") of a decade of 厚积 (hòu jī, "deep accumulation") in ADC chemistry. The drug accumulated across DESTINY-Breast01 through 05, each trial sharpening the indication. Similarly, Ailux's fundraise is a 厚积薄发 (hòu jī bó fā) moment: years of partnership revenue releasing into a first external round. The accumulation is the work. The release is the signal that the work was real.

CONCEPT TO HOLD

The Gatekeeper's Momentum

The FDA's gatekeeper function is bifurcated: the operational layer maintains momentum while the strategic layer is in freefall. An NDA with a PDUFA date is safe. An IND for a novel mechanism that needs pre-IND guidance is exposed. Know which layer your program depends on. That's where the risk lives.
