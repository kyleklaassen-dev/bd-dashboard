FDA Approval — Vepdegestrant (Veppanu) — May 1, 2026

FDA Approves First PROTAC Drug: Vepdegestrant Marks a Modality Milestone for Targeted Protein Degradation

The FDA approved vepdegestrant (Veppanu, Arvinas/Pfizer) on May 1, 2026 for ESR1-mutated, ER+/HER2− advanced breast cancer — making it the first approved PROTAC (proteolysis-targeting chimera) therapy in history. PROTACs work by recruiting the cell's own ubiquitin-proteasome system (UPS) to tag and destroy disease-causing proteins, rather than simply blocking them with a traditional inhibitor. The drug degrades the estrogen receptor (ER), achieving more complete target suppression than competitive ER antagonists like fulvestrant.

Why this matters beyond breast cancer: the approval establishes that protein degradation is a viable regulatory pathway, opening the door for PROTAC strategies in other indications — including autoimmune disease (degrading transcription factors like RORγt or STAT3 that drive inflammatory pathways and are classically considered "undruggable" by conventional antibodies or small molecules). For Ailux, this is a watch-it-closely development: PROTAC-based autoimmune approaches are still 5–8 years from clinical readout, but the platform's approval legitimization may attract capital and partnership interest in degrader-based I&I assets.

FDA Approval — Sonrotoclax (Beqalzi) — May 13, 2026

Sonrotoclax (Beqalzi) Approved for Mantle Cell Lymphoma: Next-Gen BCL-2 Gains Ground

AstraZeneca's sonrotoclax (Beqalzi) received FDA approval on May 13, 2026 for relapsed/refractory mantle cell lymphoma (MCL). This is a next-generation BCL-2 inhibitor with differentiated potency vs. venetoclax, the current BCL-2 standard. It targets the BCL-2 BH3-binding groove with higher selectivity and without the venetoclax-associated thrombocytopenia that requires dose interruption. While directly applicable to hematologic oncology, BCL-2 inhibition has emerging relevance in autoimmune disease: plasma cells — the drivers of autoantibody production in diseases like SLE, gMG, and pemphigus — are BCL-2-dependent for survival. BCL-2 inhibitors are being explored in refractory autoimmune plasma cell dyscrasias, and next-gen agents with better tolerability profiles could find a niche in the immune reset space that Ailux's ACE program occupies.

PROTACs: Targeted Protein Degradation vs. Inhibition — Why "Destroy" Beats "Block" in Some Contexts

A PROTAC (proteolysis-targeting chimera) is a bifunctional molecule with two warheads: one binds the target protein; the other recruits an E3 ubiquitin ligase. The E3 ligase tags the target with ubiquitin chains — marking it for destruction by the proteasome, the cell's own protein disposal machine. The PROTAC then dissociates and is recycled to degrade more target protein, unlike a conventional inhibitor which must continuously occupy its target.

Why does this matter vs. a classic inhibitor? Three reasons: (1) Catalytic mechanism — one PROTAC molecule can degrade many copies of the target; lower drug concentrations are required for effect. (2) "Undruggable" targets — proteins like transcription factors (STAT3, RORγt in inflammation) have no obvious active site for a small molecule to block; PROTACs bind any accessible surface region. (3) Complete target elimination — instead of 90% inhibition (leaving 10% active target), degradation achieves >95% protein loss. The tradeoff: linker chemistry is complex, oral bioavailability is challenging, and off-target degradation can create toxicity. Vepdegestrant's approval proves the FDA will accept the modality when benefit/risk is clear. Watch for I&I PROTAC programs targeting RORγt (Th17 driver) and STAT3 (JAK/STAT inflammation) in the next wave of platform development.

"Strike first to gain the upper hand"
Literal meaning: "First act, control people." The idiom describes a strategic advantage gained by acting before an adversary can move.

Origin: From the Shǐjì (史记, Shǐ jì, "Records of the Grand Historian") — a general advises that in war, the side that strikes first controls the battle.

BD application: The UCB–Candid deal is a textbook 先发制人 (xiān fā zhì rén) move — UCB acquired Candid's BCMA TCE platform before clinical proof-of-concept in autoimmune was established, securing the position before competitors could. For Ailux: the same logic applies to partnership timing. A deal signed on preclinical differentiation data commands a better structure than one signed after Phase 1 data is available (when every large pharma company is watching). First movers who define the terms win.

Roche's Gazyva (Obinutuzumab) Under FDA Review for SLE: CD20 Antibody Enters Lupus Race

Roche's Gazyva (obinutuzumab, a glycoengineered anti-CD20 IgG1) has been accepted by FDA for review as a treatment for systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), with a PDUFA decision expected by December 2026. Obinutuzumab is a next-gen rituximab with type II CD20 binding that drives superior ADCC and direct cell death vs. rituximab. The SLE application is based on Phase 3 NOBILITY extension data showing sustained reductions in lupus nephritis activity.

For Ailux's ACE program: approved CD20 antibodies in lupus nephritis establish B-cell depletion as a proven clinical strategy in SLE. But CD20 depletes B-cells without touching BCMA+ plasma cells — the long-lived autoantibody factories that survive CD20 depletion and drive relapse. Ailux's trispecific covers both. If Gazyva is approved, it validates the market and simultaneously highlights the residual unmet need that CD20-only depletion cannot address.
