FcRn: argenx’s $1.3 Billion Quarter and the Franchise That Won’t Stop Expanding

FOCUS AREA: FcRn · AUTOIMMUNE IgG DISEASES

On May 7, argenx reported Q1 2026 financial results that confirmed VYVGART’s position as the dominant FcRn inhibitor franchise globally. Product net sales reached $1.298 billion for the quarter, representing 63% year-over-year growth from $790 million in Q1 2025. The company posted a profit of $366 million, more than doubling the $169 million earned in the same period last year. Cash and current financial assets totaled $4.9 billion as of March 31, 2026.

The commercial story is now well established — VYVGART is approved in generalized myasthenia gravis (gMG) and chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy (CIDP) globally, and in primary immune thrombocytopenia (ITP) in Japan. What matters for the BD landscape is what comes next. The PDUFA target action date for the seronegative gMG expansion (AChR-Ab-negative, covering MuSK+, LRP4+, and triple seronegative patients) was May 10, 2026. Positive topline data from ADAPT OCULUS, presented at AAN, support a planned sBLA submission for ocular MG. If both expansions are approved, VYVGART becomes the first and only therapy labeled across all MG subtypes.

But the real signal for Ailux is the pipeline beyond efgartigimod. The ALKIVIA study topline readout in idiopathic inflammatory myopathies (myositis) is expected in Q3 2026 — the first registrational test of FcRn blockade in a rheumatologic indication. A registrational study in Graves’ disease is expected to initiate in 2026, expanding the FcRn thesis into thyroid-driven autoimmunity. argenx is also progressing empasiprubart (anti-complement C2) into registrational studies in multifocal motor neuropathy (MMN) and CIDP, with the EMPASSION MMN readout expected Q4 2026. Two next-generation FcRn molecules — ARGX-213 (Phase 3-ready) and ARGX-124 (Phase 1) — are being advanced to sustain the franchise beyond efgartigimod’s patent life.

IL-4Rα: Rademikibart Posts Best-in-Class Numbers Against Dupilumab

FOCUS AREA: IL-4Rα · ATOPY (AD, PRURIGO, ALLERGIC ASTHMA)

The Phase 3 RADIANT-AD results for rademikibart, presented at the AAD 2026 Annual Meeting in Denver on March 28, represent the most compelling dataset yet produced by a next-generation IL-4R-alpha inhibitor. Connect Biopharma (San Diego) and its Chinese partner Simcere Pharmaceutical reported 52-week data from 259 adolescents and adults with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis that exceeded dupilumab’s historical benchmarks across every key endpoint.

The primary endpoint at week 16 showed 47.7% of rademikibart-treated patients achieving IGA 0/1 versus 17.6% on placebo. But the 52-week maintenance data are where rademikibart separates itself: 96.6% of patients achieved EASI-75, 85.3% achieved EASI-90, and 87.1% maintained IGA 0/1. These response rates approach near-maximal clearance in approximately 90% of treated patients — a durability profile that dupilumab’s pivotal trials did not demonstrate. Critically, the conjunctivitis rate was not differentiated from placebo through the initial 16-week double-blind phase and remained low through 52 weeks, addressing what has been a persistent tolerability issue with IL-4R-alpha blockade.

Rademikibart is a fully human monoclonal antibody that binds a distinct epitope on IL-4R-alpha with approximately 2-fold higher affinity than dupilumab (Sanofi/Regeneron). Connect Biopharma has previously shown in a Phase 2b study that rademikibart dosed every 4 weeks performed comparably to every 2 weeks — an important convenience signal for a chronic-dosing indication. Simcere holds exclusive Greater China rights and has a pending NDA with the NMPA. Connect retains rights outside China and is positioned to seek a global partner.

BD signal: Connect Biopharma (NASDAQ: CNTB) is a clinical-stage company with a market capitalization well below its data profile. The RADIANT-AD results create a clear licensing or acquisition narrative for any pharma company seeking to challenge Sanofi/Regeneron’s dupilumab franchise — currently the top-selling biologic in dermatology. With rademikibart also in development for asthma and COPD, a comprehensive deal could span three blockbuster indications. The Q4W dosing optionality and lower conjunctivitis rate are differentiators that a BD team can price.

TL1A: New Mechanism Enters the IBD Ring — Accro’s RIPK2 Inhibitor

FOCUS AREA: TL1A · IBD (UC + CROHN’S)

While the TL1A class consolidates around its Phase 3 leaders, a mechanistically distinct entrant emerged on May 19: Accro Bioscience, a Suzhou- and New York-based clinical-stage company, closed a $50 million Series C financing led by OrbiMed. The capital will fund a US Phase 2b trial of AC-101, a selective oral inhibitor of receptor-interacting protein kinase 2 (RIPK2), for moderate-to-severe ulcerative colitis. Participating investors include TCG Crossover (TCGX), LAV, Cenova Capital, and existing backers Shenzhen Capital Group and Oriza Holdings.

RIPK2 sits in the NOD1/NOD2 innate immune signaling pathway — mechanistically orthogonal to TL1A, IL-23, and JAK. Dysregulation of NOD/RIPK2 signaling is implicated in mucosal barrier breakdown and inflammatory amplification in IBD. AC-101 has completed Phase 1 studies in healthy volunteers in Australia and China with a favorable safety and PK/PD profile, plus a Phase 1b/2a proof-of-concept study in Chinese patients with moderate-to-severe UC. The compound has received US FDA IND clearance for the Phase 2b trial. No RIPK2-specific inhibitor has been approved for any disease, positioning AC-101 as a potential first-in-class therapy.

The OrbiMed lead is notable — OrbiMed also led Windward Bio’s $165 million round for the long-acting TSLP antibody WIN378 announced May 4. OrbiMed’s willingness to fund two novel-mechanism programs in I&I within the same month signals confidence in the breadth of unmet need beyond the TL1A/IL-23 axis. Accro’s platform focuses on regulatory cell death and inflammation, suggesting additional pipeline candidates beyond AC-101 in other immune-mediated diseases.

BD signal: AC-101 does not compete directly with Ailux’s TL1A program — it is a different mechanism attacking the same disease. But its existence compresses the timeline for differentiation. If RIPK2 inhibition shows clinical activity in UC, the IBD treatment paradigm becomes even more crowded. For Ailux’s TL1A asset, epitope-differentiated and anti-fibrotic-biased positioning becomes not just a differentiator but a survival strategy. The fibrosis-specific biology is what no small-molecule oral can replicate.

T-Cell Engineering: The EULAR Countdown — What to Watch June 3–6

FOCUS AREA: T-CELL ENGINEERING (ACE) · IMMUNE RESET (SLE, MYOSITIS)

Cabaletta Bio’s Q1 2026 earnings call on May 14 confirmed the most anticipated data catalysts in autoimmune cell therapy: complete Phase 1/2 clinical data from the RESET-SLE and RESET-SSc (systemic sclerosis) trials will be presented at EULAR 2026 in London, June 3–6. The SLE data (poster POS0698) drops at 9:30 a.m. BST on Thursday, June 4. Separately, initial preconditioning-free (PC-free) data from the lowest-dose SLE cohort is expected in the first half of 2026 — potentially at or around EULAR itself.

The company is now enrolling its pivotal myositis trial to support a planned rese-cel BLA submission the following year. The financial position is sound: Cabaletta completed a $150 million financing earlier in 2026. With the Cellares automated manufacturing platform validated in clinical patients (presented at ASGCT 2026 on May 14), the commercial-scale bottleneck that has limited autologous CAR-T in autoimmune disease begins to loosen. The 10-year Cellares supply agreement is designed for thousands of doses annually with minimal capital investment.

For context on the broader competitive landscape: the RESOLUTION trial at Norton Cancer Institute is evaluating ALLO-329, an allogeneic CAR-T product targeting both CD19 (B cells) and CD70 (T cells) in SLE, myositis, and scleroderma. This dual-targeting allogeneic approach represents a fundamentally different strategy from Cabaletta’s autologous CD19-only product. The EULAR data will establish whether autologous, PC-free CD19 CAR-T achieves sufficient immune reset depth in lupus to justify the logistic complexity — or whether the field pivots toward allogeneic off-the-shelf alternatives.

BD signal: EULAR is the near-term catalyst to watch. If the RESET-SLE complete dataset shows durable drug-free remission comparable to the early Erlangen CD19 CAR-T data, deal valuations for autoimmune CAR-T programs will reprice upward. Cabaletta’s choice of second pivotal indication (expected post-EULAR) will signal where they see the fastest regulatory path. For Ailux’s broader T-cell engineering interest, the PC-free SLE data is the swing variable — it determines whether autoimmune CAR-T stays a specialty product or becomes a scalable therapy.

CONCEPT TO HOLD

Value Chain Analysis: Who Captures Margin When the Mechanism Is Commoditized?

Michael Porter’s value chain framework (MF720, Corporate Strategy) decomposes a firm’s activities into primary and support functions to identify where economic value is created and captured. Apply it to today’s FcRn landscape: argenx, UCB, and Johnson & Johnson all inhibit the same receptor, yet argenx captures >80% of revenue. The mechanism is commoditized; the margin is not. Where does argenx’s value chain advantage sit? Not in discovery (all three have viable molecules) but in indication sequencing (first-mover in gMG, then CIDP, now myositis), delivery modality (IV, SC, autoinjector pipeline), and commercial infrastructure (17 consecutive quarters of growth with a dedicated rare-disease sales force).

The lesson for Ailux: when evaluating crowded target classes — whether TL1A, IL-4R-alpha, or FcRn — ask not just “does the molecule work?” but “where in the value chain will we build margin that a competitor cannot replicate?” For TL1A specifically, Ailux’s potential advantage is in the epitope-fibrosis nexus at the discovery stage, but sustaining that advantage requires a value chain strategy through clinical development (indication selection) and commercialization (partner selection). The molecule is the entry ticket. The value chain is the moat.

CHENGYU · 成语 (chéngyǔ)

深入浅出

shēn rù qiǎn chū — “to go in deep and come out shallow”

Literally: enter deeply, emerge simply. Used to describe the ability to take something profoundly complex and make it accessible without losing rigor. This is the challenge of every molecule discussed today. argenx takes the complexity of FcRn biology — neonatal receptor recycling, IgG half-life kinetics, complement interplay — and delivers it as a single subcutaneous injection that a myasthenia gravis patient self-administers at home. Connect Biopharma takes the intricate epitope engineering of IL-4R-alpha blockade and distills it into a Q4W dosing schedule. Accro Bioscience reduces the NOD/RIPK2 innate immune cascade to a once-daily oral pill. The science goes in deep (深入, shēn rù). The product comes out simple (浅出, qiǎn chū). Depth of understanding, clarity of delivery — that is what separates a program that publishes from a program that treats patients.

Watch the May 10 PDUFA outcome for seronegative gMG — if approved, it reshapes the MG treatment algorithm overnight and pressures every competitor in the space to broaden their own label ambitions.
