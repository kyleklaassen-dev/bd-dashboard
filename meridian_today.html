Seventy-four days. That is how long a single molecule of XmAb942 persists in the human bloodstream after one subcutaneous injection — long enough that a patient with ulcerative colitis could receive maintenance dosing four times a year. Xencor reported those Phase 1 results at Digestive Disease Week earlier this month, and the number deserves more than a glance, because it reveals what the TL1A class is becoming: not just a new mechanism, but a new pharmacokinetic paradigm. The first-generation TL1A antibodies — tulisokibart, duvakitug — dose every four weeks. XmAb942 doses every twelve. And Xencor is already building the next step: XmAb412, a TL1A × IL-23p19 bispecific, entering its first-in-human study in Q3.

Meanwhile, the week delivered a different kind of lesson from the FcRn space. Immunovant formally discontinued batoclimab after dual Phase 3 failures in thyroid eye disease, paying $39 million to exit and pivoting entirely to IMVT-1402. And Fate Therapeutics showed at ASGCT that its iPSC-derived CAR-T product FT819 can drive B-cell remodeling in SLE patients without conditioning chemotherapy — 2 of 3 patients achieving lupus low disease activity state from a single dose. The tea is steep. The engineering is deep.

TL1A: The 74-Day Half-Life and the Bispecific on Its Heels

FOCUS AREA: TL1A · IBD (UC + CROHN’S)

Xencor’s final Phase 1 results for XmAb942 were presented at DDW on May 4 in Chicago (BioSpace). The pooled single-dose cohort analysis produced an estimated terminal half-life of 74.1 days — more than double that of any first-generation TL1A antibody in clinical development. This pharmacokinetic profile supports the Q12W subcutaneous maintenance dosing schedule now being tested in the global Phase 2b XENITH-UC study, which is enrolling approximately 220 patients across three active treatment arms and one placebo arm.

The quantitative pharmacology modeling is where the competitive separation becomes stark. During induction, XmAb942 is predicted to achieve greater than 99% TL1A inhibition in 86% of patients, compared to 31% and 40% for first-generation antibodies. During maintenance, greater than 90% TL1A inhibition is predicted in 90% of patients on the Q12W schedule — versus 60% and 68% for the Q4W-dosed competitors. All dose levels and routes of administration were well tolerated: no serious or severe treatment-emergent adverse events, no discontinuations.

The second announcement is equally consequential for the class. XmAb412, Xencor’s TL1A × IL-23p19 bispecific antibody built on the novel XenLock format, will enter its first-in-human dose-escalation study in healthy participants during Q3 2026. XmAb412 binds TL1A with single-digit picomolar affinity and IL-23p19 with sub-picomolar affinity. The 1+1 format avoids large immune complex formation associated with 2+2 architectures. In non-human primates, XmAb412 achieved a half-life exceeding 20 days, with predicted human half-life between 60 and 70 days. If both pathways can be blocked with a single molecule dosed subcutaneously every 12 weeks, the simplification of IBD treatment would be substantial.

FcRn × IGF1R: Immunovant Exits TED, Consolidates Around IMVT-1402

FOCUS AREAS: FcRn · AUTOIMMUNE IgG DISEASES / IGF1R · THYROID EYE DISEASE

Immunovant has formally discontinued batoclimab, its first-generation FcRn inhibitor, paying $39 million to terminate the program after both Phase 3 GO studies in thyroid eye disease failed to meet the primary endpoint of a 2 mm or greater reduction in proptosis at Week 24 (Fierce Biotech). The discontinuation is not surprising — argenx had previously exited its own VYVGART TED program — but the $39 million exit payment to partner HanAll Biopharma underscores the real cost of pursuing an indication where the mechanism may not be sufficient.

The pattern is now clear: FcRn blockade alone does not produce adequate proptosis reduction in TED. This is because TED pathology involves not just circulating anti-TSH receptor autoantibodies (the IgG component FcRn blockers address) but also direct IGF-1R-mediated orbital fibroblast activation and hyaluronan deposition. Teprotumumab (Amgen’s TEPEZZA), which targets IGF-1R directly, remains the only approved therapy specifically addressing the orbital tissue remodeling that drives proptosis. The UK granted TEPEZZA marketing authorization as recently as May 2025, expanding its global footprint.

Immunovant’s strategic pivot is instructive. The company is now fully consolidated around IMVT-1402, a subcutaneous anti-FcRn antibody with six ongoing studies across Graves’ disease, difficult-to-treat rheumatoid arthritis, myasthenia gravis, CIDP, Sjogren’s disease, and cutaneous lupus erythematosus. The Graves’ disease program is notable — it targets the upstream autoantibody pathology rather than the downstream orbital tissue effects, learning from the TED failure by staying in the IgG-mediated space rather than crossing into structural remodeling territory.

BD signal: The dual failure of FcRn inhibitors in TED effectively validates IGF-1R as the mechanistically correct target for orbital remodeling. Viridian Therapeutics, which is advancing its own anti-IGF-1R program, sees its competitive position strengthen with each FcRn exit from the TED space. For Ailux’s FcRn program, the lesson is one of indication selection: FcRn blockade works where IgG clearance is the rate-limiting step (MG, CIDP, ITP) and fails where tissue-level pathology exceeds what IgG reduction can address.

T-Cell Engineering: Fate’s iPSC CAR-T Achieves Lupus Response Without Chemotherapy

FOCUS AREA: T-CELL ENGINEERING (ACE) · IMMUNE RESET (SLE, MYOSITIS)

At the American Society of Gene and Cell Therapy (ASGCT) Annual Meeting in Boston on May 11, Fate Therapeutics presented clinical data from its Phase 1 study of FT819 — an off-the-shelf, CD19-targeted, iPSC-derived CAR T-cell product — in patients with moderate-to-severe systemic lupus erythematosus (BioSpace). In Regimen B, with a data cutoff of April 9, 2026, a single dose of FT819 without conditioning chemotherapy and in the presence of background therapy produced meaningful clinical responses at dose level 1: 3 of 3 patients achieved SRI-4 (SLE Responder Index), and 2 of 3 achieved lupus low disease activity state (LLDAS).

The mechanistic data are equally important. B cells in both peripheral blood and secondary lymphoid tissue (assessed via nasopharyngeal swabs) were significantly depleted. B-cell receptor sequencing revealed that major B-cell clones were depleted by an average of 79%, with the depletion lasting up to 12 months following a single treatment. The B-cell repertoire shifted toward less class-switched configurations — a signature of genuine immune remodeling rather than transient suppression. Patient treatment at the higher dose of 900 million cells (dose level 2) has been initiated.

Fate also previewed preclinical data for FT839, a next-generation dual-CAR T-cell therapy targeting CD19 and CD38. In rheumatoid arthritis and SLE disease samples, FT839 depleted over 99% of B cells, plasmablasts, and plasma cells, and more than 90% of activated CD4+ and CD8+ T cells — while sparing non-activated T cells. The Sword and Shield technology enhanced persistence approximately 60-fold in HLA-mismatched allogeneic settings while reducing product-specific immune cell responses by approximately 900-fold, reducing the need for conditioning chemotherapy. This dual-CAR approach addresses a key limitation of CD19-only therapies: long-lived plasma cells, which are BCMA/CD38-dependent and CD19-negative, can survive CD19-targeted depletion and continue producing autoantibodies.

BD signal: Fate’s conditioning-free iPSC CAR-T data change the scalability equation for autoimmune cell therapy. If immune reset can be achieved without lymphodepleting chemotherapy using an off-the-shelf product, the addressable patient population expands from last-resort refractory cases to earlier-line intervention. The EULAR data from Cabaletta Bio (rese-cel, June 3–6) will provide the autologous comparator. The strategic question for Ailux is whether the field converges on allogeneic off-the-shelf (Fate’s approach) or autologous engineered (Cabaletta’s approach) — and whether dual-targeting (CD19+CD38 or CD19+BCMA) becomes the new standard.

TSLP: The Bispecific Frontier Opens as Windward Approaches Phase 3

FOCUS AREA: TSLP · RESPIRATORY (ASTHMA, COPD)

Two developments in the TSLP space this month crystallize the competitive dynamics. First, Windward Bio announced a $165 million crossover financing on May 4 (Fierce Biotech), led by Novo Holdings with participation from OrbiMed and others. The capital will advance WIN378 — a long-acting anti-TSLP antibody with potential twice-yearly dosing — into Phase 3 in asthma (expected Q4 2026) and fund the Phase 2 SIRIUS study in COPD (expected to start before June 2026). WIN378 directly challenges Amgen’s tezepelumab (TEZSPIRE), which requires monthly dosing.

Second, Aclaris Therapeutics is running parallel Phase 1b proof-of-concept trials of ATI-052, a bispecific antibody that simultaneously inhibits TSLP and IL-4R-alpha (BioSpace). The atopic dermatitis trial initiated in January 2026 and the asthma trial in February 2026, with top-line results for both expected H2 2026. ATI-052 showed at least a 26-day half-life and a favorable safety profile up to 720 mg in Phase 1a. The logic is appealing: TSLP sits at the top of the inflammatory cascade and IL-4R-alpha blocks the downstream type 2 effector arm. If dual blockade produces additive or synergistic clinical benefit, ATI-052 would represent a category-defining asset spanning the TSLP and IL-4R-alpha areas simultaneously.

BD signal: The TSLP space is bifurcating into two strategies: longer-acting monospecifics (Windward’s WIN378, twice-yearly dosing) and dual-mechanism bispecifics (Aclaris’s ATI-052, TSLP + IL-4R-alpha). Both threaten tezepelumab’s market position from different angles — convenience and depth, respectively. For Ailux, the ATI-052 program is particularly relevant as it bridges the TSLP and IL-4R-alpha focus areas, suggesting that future competitive mapping should account for cross-area bispecifics.

CONCEPT TO HOLD

Interim Analysis and Alpha Spending: When an Early Look Costs More Than It Reveals

Summit Therapeutics learned this week what every clinical development student encounters in MF705 (Clinical Development): an interim analysis is not free information. The independent data monitoring committee reviewed progression-free survival data from HARMONi-3 — Summit’s Phase 3 trial testing the PD-1×VEGF bispecific ivonescimab against Merck’s KEYTRUDA in first-line squamous NSCLC — and the efficacy signal did not cross the pre-specified statistical boundary. The trial continues, but Summit’s stock fell 27%.

The framework at work is the alpha-spending function (Lan-DeMets, O’Brien-Fleming, or Pocock boundaries), which governs how the overall Type I error rate (alpha = 0.05) is distributed across planned analyses. An O’Brien-Fleming boundary — the most conservative and most commonly used — requires an overwhelmingly strong signal at early interim looks (often p < 0.001) to preserve most of the alpha for the final analysis. In practical terms, failing to cross at an interim is not the same as failing the trial. It means the effect size was not large enough at that sample fraction to meet an intentionally stringent bar.

The market’s 27% reaction reveals a gap between statistical design and investor interpretation. The trial was powered for a final PFS readout in H2 2026. The interim was added to potentially accelerate a regulatory filing — an optionality play. Missing it means Summit follows the original timeline, not that ivonescimab doesn’t work. But the market prices binary outcomes, not conditional probabilities. For anyone evaluating trial design — whether at Ailux or in an MF705 exam — the lesson is that adding an interim analysis creates two risks: statistical (alpha consumed) and reputational (perception of failure). The decision to add one should weigh both.

CHENGYU · 成语 (chéngyǔ)

厚积薄发

hòu jī bó fā — “accumulate thickly, release thinly”

Literally: build up deeply, then release in a thin, powerful stream. Used to describe years of quiet investment producing sudden, concentrated impact. This is the engineering philosophy on display across today’s stories. Xencor spent years developing XmAb half-life extension technology — the Xtend Fc modifications, the immunogenicity optimization — and the output is deceptively simple: one injection every twelve weeks. Fate Therapeutics built an entire iPSC manufacturing platform, clonal master cell banks, and the Sword and Shield persistence technology, and the clinical output is equally spare: a single dose, no chemotherapy, durable immune reset. The accumulation (厚积, hòu jī) happens in the engineering. The release (薄发, bó fā) happens in the clinic. The patient sees only the thin edge of years of work — and that is exactly the point.

Watch Xencor’s XENITH-UC blinded interim analysis around year-end 2026 — if the Q12W dosing schedule holds clinical remission, it resets the TL1A competitive clock for every program behind it.
