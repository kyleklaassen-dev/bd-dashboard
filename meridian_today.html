The TL1A×IL-23p19 bispecific went from white space to a crowded field in under eighteen months, and the entire valuation ladder for the class is now being set by deals struck before a single patient has been dosed in IBD. At least eight programs exist — SIM0709 (Simcere/Boehringer), CLD-423 (Caldera/Qyuns), MT-251 (Mirador), HY8931 (Newsoara), HBM2001 (Harbour), BA2201 (Boan), SAB06 (Santa Ana), plus Xencor's XmAb412 and Earendil's EAR-2001 — and four will be in the clinic by Q4 2026. Ailux's ALX001 remains in IND-enabling studies. The first-in-class timing argument is dead, and the data confirm it on every axis except one that still matters: human efficacy.

The pricing signal is the actual news, and it is more disciplined than the headline totals suggest. Boehringer Ingelheim paid €42M upfront against €1.058B total for SIM0709 on pure preclinical data; Caldera in-licensed CLD-423 from Qyuns for $10M upfront plus 24.88% equity and up to $545M at Phase 1 entry. Read together, these two deals say the format premium is not being paid at signing — upfronts are modest and milestone-loaded. That reframes Ailux's entire ambition: the leverage is not in proving a differentiated molecule preclinically (every competitor already claims sub-0.1nM affinity and DR3 selectivity), it is in upfront magnitude and milestone front-loading, which only superior human efficacy data can unlock. The €42M figure is the floor, not the comp Ailux should price against.

Two developments this cycle sharpen the threat. First, the Elpiscience ES302 abstract at ECCO 2026 (DOP122) referenced its molecule as "ALX001" — the exact internal designation Ailux uses for its lead — describing a 2+2 symmetric, DR3-selective, sub-0.1nM bispecific. This converts ES302 from the "undisclosed second target" surveillance placeholder we had flagged into a confirmed direct TL1A×IL-23p19 competitor, and it puts a same-named, same-format molecule into the permanent public preclinical record. Ailux now carries a naming-confusion liability that must be resolved before any conference disclosure of its own data, or the differentiated trimer-disassembling mechanism gets attributed to a Chinese 2+2 symmetric molecule it does not resemble.

Second, the US preclinical players are staffing for a sale, not a clinical slog. Santa Ana Bio added Newman Yeilding as CMO in February — the J&J IL-23 architect behind Stelara and Tremfya — on top of Mike Bonney as Board Chair, who engineered Cubist's $9.5B exit to Merck. That is the same pre-transaction governance pattern Mirador showed with its Prometheus-alumni founding team and $650M+ raised. The pharma counterparty pool Ailux competes for is being approached right now by better-credentialed sellers — and the falsifier for this entire thesis is specific: if Merck's ATLAS-UC tulisokibart Phase 3 reads out negative in November, the class-validation narrative collapses, every bispecific repriced downward, and Ailux's window does not close — it disappears, along with the appetite.

Mechanism Intelligence

TL1A×IL-23p19: The Format Premium Is Paid in Human Data, Not Affinity

The bispecific blocks two non-redundant inflammatory axes at once: TL1A signaling through DR3 (driving Th1/Th17 mucosal inflammation and fibrosis) and IL-23 via its p19 subunit (sustaining pathogenic Th17 effector function), while sparing IL-12-driven Th1 immunity that p40-targeted agents like ustekinumab also hit. TL1A carries an Open Targets genetic association of 0.892 with IBD (0.876 in Crohn's, 0.838 in UC) — among the strongest human-genetics anchors in the field — and the target is LoF-tolerant in gnomAD, a reassuring systemic-blockade safety read. The patient at issue is the biologic-failure IBD population: of roughly 1,680,000 US IBD patients, 42% fail biologics and standard-of-care delivers remission in only about 22%. For them, the bispecific hypothesis is that simultaneous blockade reaches deep remission where sequential single-mechanism agents have not.

Every preclinical disclosure this cycle made the same molecular claims. Elpiscience's ES302 reported KD <0.1nM (TL1A) and <0.01nM (IL-23p19), DR3-selective blockade sparing DcR3, and pH-sensitive FcRn recycling for SC Q4W dosing. Novamab's LQ080 VHH bispecific claimed superior IFNγ inhibition versus tulisokibart and 2–3× extended NHP half-life. Simcere's SIM0709 AAI 2025 data showed superiority over the afimkibart-plus-guselkumab combination in colitis models. The implication is that preclinical differentiation is now table stakes — when six molecules all claim best-in-class affinity and DR3 selectivity, none of them is differentiated. Ailux's trimer-disassembling TL1A mechanism is genuinely distinct architecturally, but it converts to deal value only if it produces a remission delta in humans that the symmetric 2+2 designs cannot.

The naming collision compounds the problem. Because the ES302 abstract used "ALX001," the public record now contains a same-named molecule of a different format and mechanism. If Ailux discloses its own preclinical or Phase 1 data under that designation, the differentiation narrative — trimer disassembly versus simple bivalent neutralization — is at risk of being conflated with a competitor's 2+2 symmetric package in literature search, KOL reference, and partner diligence.

BD LENS

Resolve the ALX001/ES302 name conflict with IP counsel before any public data disclosure; assign a clean clinical designation. Frame all partner diligence material around the trimer-disassembling mechanism as the one architectural claim no symmetric competitor can make — and commit to a head-to-head preclinical comparator versus a 2+2 benchmark before first conference disclosure.

TL1A Monospecific: Tulisokibart Sets — and Complicates — the Ceiling

Tulisokibart is Merck's anti-TL1A monoclonal, the most advanced asset against the target. Its ARTEMIS-UC Phase 2 delivered 26% clinical remission versus 1% placebo at Week 12 in the broad population, and 32% versus 11% in the TNFSF15 CDx-positive subgroup — but endoscopic improvement in CDx-positive patients did not reach significance, failing to confirm the companion diagnostic's clinical utility. The APOLLO-CD Phase 2a added 49.1% clinical remission and 26% endoscopic response at Week 12 in Crohn's. These are the monospecific TL1A benchmarks a bispecific must visibly exceed to justify a differentiation premium.

The CDx miss is the single most useful asset Ailux has for positioning. If Merck's TL1A program ends up label-restricted to a biomarker-defined subgroup, a CDx-free bispecific addressing the full IBD population becomes the broader commercial proposition by default. We put ATLAS-UC reading positive at roughly 68% (base 50, +15 Phase 2 separation 26% vs 1%, +10 genetic validation 0.89, −7 Phase 2→3 dilution and CDx ambiguity → 68%). A positive readout validates the mechanism and lifts every bispecific; a failure is a class-level event that reprices the whole field, including ALX001.

BD LENS

Build the partner deck around CDx-free, full-population coverage as the bispecific's commercial edge over tulisokibart. Time the most consequential pharma conversations to land before the ~Nov 2026 ATLAS-UC topline — after it, a positive readout raises the asking price and a negative one closes the door.

BD & Deal Watch

The defining transaction remains Boehringer's SIM0709 license — €42M upfront, €1.058B total, executed entirely on preclinical data. As a privately held, self-funded entity with €14.0B H1 2025 net sales and no disclosed runway constraint, BI sets SIM0709's timeline by internal prioritization alone, with FIH guided for Q3 2026. The strategic logic: BI bought class entry at a modest upfront and is paying for success in milestones. For Ailux, this is the floor — any deal carrying Phase 2 human efficacy should command a materially higher upfront than €42M, not a multiple of the headline total.

Caldera's structure tells the complementary story. The $10M-upfront/$545M-total in-license from Qyuns, with first subjects dosed January 2026 and a $112.5M crossover-syndicate raise (Omega, Wellington, Janus Henderson), funds CLD-423 through Phase 1 completion without near-term partnering pressure. CLD-423 is the first TL1A×IL-23p19 bispecific in the clinic — its Q3 2026 PK/safety readout is the first human data point for the entire format class, and it will anchor what every subsequent counterparty believes a Phase 1 bispecific is worth.

The China out-licensing precedents stack the same way. Boan's FY2025 net income collapsed 90% to RMB 7.14M with BA2201 China IND guided for 2026 and active MNC discussions underway — a cash-pressured owner of a mechanism-relevant asset that may accept sub-optimal terms, setting a public preclinical valuation floor when it signs. Leads Biolabs, by contrast, raised ~$189M in its July 2025 HKEX IPO and licensed LBL-047 to Dianthus for up to $1B ex-China — its TL1A×IL-23p40 LBL-053 (note: p40, not p19, so it sacrifices the IL-12 sparing that defines the p19 thesis) will follow the retain-China/out-license-global template when IND-ready, with no funding pressure to discount.

On FTO: the anti-TL1A estate spans 30 patent families, densest at Prometheus Biosciences (8 families, expiries 2037–2045), with Cantai holding 2. Any buyer's first diligence question is freedom-to-operate against the Prometheus/Merck estate — Ailux should pre-clear this before approaching counterparties. And the orilanolimab litigation is the structural lesson: the Delaware Chancery Court awarded $311M+ cumulative damages against Alexion/AstraZeneca for terminating a program on merger-synergy grounds rather than science. Ailux must write enforceable CRE provisions and milestone definitions into any out-license or acquisition agreement.

BD LENS

Pre-clear FTO against the Prometheus/Merck TL1A estate before any counterparty diligence opens. Do not benchmark Ailux's ask against SIM0709's €42M upfront — benchmark against what a Phase 2-validated asset commands, and structure CRE/milestone clauses to the orilanolimab standard. Approach Takeda and Roche first; hold AbbVie until post-ABBV-701 (~Oct 2026).
