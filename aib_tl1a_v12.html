The landscape in brief

Four anti-TL1A monotherapies are in Phase 3: tulisokibart (Merck), afimkibart (Roche), duvakitug (Sanofi/Teva), and SPY002 (Spyre) in Phase 2. Anti-TL1A is clinically validated but not yet approved. The unresolved question is whether combining TL1A blockade with IL-23p19 blockade in a single molecule produces additive efficacy beyond the best monotherapy; no bispecific has reported a human efficacy readout. A first additive-efficacy result, paired with a manufacturable long-acting subcutaneous profile and a clean safety dataset, enters a deal range set by Merck's $10.8B purchase of Prometheus and Roche's $7.1B purchase of Telavant.

State of validation: IL-23p19 monotherapy is approved and durable in IBD; TL1A monotherapy is validated in Phase 2 and currently in Phase 3; the dual-mechanism bispecific is unproven and, in the clinic, uncrowded. The opportunity is the gap between today's ~15-20% deep-remission ceiling and the 30-40% dual blockade is hypothesized to reach.

Field view. Independent commentary (ACG review, 2024) describes anti-TL1A as a credible new class while noting remission rates remain modest and durability and positioning are unproven — the case for a mechanism that lifts the efficacy ceiling rather than adding another single-pathway option. TD Cowen's 2025 GI Outlook reaches the same conclusion from the sell-side: TL1A monotherapy data are 'more similar than different' across programs, and combinations may be needed in TNF-experienced patients — the exact gap a TL1A×IL-23p19 bispecific targets.

Both diseases share a remission ceiling: the best agents reach ~15-20% deep remission and 40-50% of patients fail or lose response within five years (secondary loss ~10-20%/yr). The diseases differ in what is unmet: UC turns on mucosal/endoscopic healing and the colectomy endpoint; CD adds fibrostenotic and fistulizing complications that no current agent durably resolves — and where TL1A's role in fibro-stromal remodeling is mechanistically most relevant. A dual-mechanism asset should be read against both.

Why a bispecific. Anti-TL1A monotherapy leaves an IL-23-dependent Th17 escape route open (Section 3); IL-23p19 monotherapy leaves a non-overlapping Th1/IFN-γ route. One molecule covering both is the mechanism-level response to a ceiling single pathways have not broken — the same logic behind AbbVie pairing ABBV-701 (anti-TL1A) with SKYRIZI (IL-23) as a two-drug regimen.

3 · The scientific thesis

Engagement vs efficacy

Anti-TL1A antibodies achieve ~90% suppression of free soluble TL1A (tulisokibart ~90%, afimkibart ~92%), yet clinical remission is ~26%. In ARTEMIS-UC, tulisokibart produced 26% remission vs 1% placebo at Week 12, and 32% vs 11% in a genetic-biomarker-selected subgroup. The antibody binds and neutralizes its target; the disease persists in roughly three-quarters of patients. Closing that gap is a problem the field has to solve.

The escape pathway

TL1A blockade leaves IL-23-dependent Th17 activation and epithelial-restitution failure untouched. Anti-TL1A non-responders escape predominantly through Th17 routes; anti-IL-23 non-responders escape through Th1 / IL-12-independent IFN-γ — two largely non-overlapping maps. Afimkibart's serum proteomics show the converse: it downregulates Th17, myeloid and fibrotic pathways, confirming TL1A's fibrotic reach and where a second mechanism remains needed.

Why IL-23p19, and what is already proven

IL-23p19 closes the Th17 route while preserving IL-12/Th1 immunity (unlike p40), and it is already validated in IBD: mirikizumab (Omvoh) approved UC 2023 and CD 2025 with four-year durability, risankizumab (Skyrizi) approved CD and UC, guselkumab approved. Of the two arms, IL-23p19 is fully de-risked, TL1A is partially de-risked (positive Phase 2, in Phase 3, not approved), and the combination is the only unproven element. The remaining work is the additive-efficacy readout, not validation of either pathway.

Testable conclusion. If the engagement-to-remission gap is driven by a non-overlapping IL-23-dependent escape route, dual blockade should convert engagement into remission — the 30-40% deep-remission hypothesis. It is falsifiable: if dual blockade does not beat the best monospecific, the thesis fails. RO7837195 (p40×TL1A) and MT-251 are the first programs positioned to test it; neither has reported additive efficacy.

This is a hypothesis the biology supports, not a validated companion diagnostic.

A TL1A/IL-23 asset competes in two arenas. Target space is the other TL1A and TL1A×IL-23 programs above. Indication space is the full IBD armamentarium a prescriber and payer choose among — and it is increasingly oral.

Why this matters. Oral small molecules (JAK, S1P, TYK2) and cheap anti-TNF biosimilars mean a quarterly subcutaneous bispecific must justify itself on efficacy and durability, not convenience alone — a daily pill is hard to beat on patient preference. The strategic read for the dual-blockade thesis: its defensible claim is depth and durability of remission an oral cannot match, in the refractory population where orals and single-pathway biologics have failed. Adjacent novel targets (e.g., next-generation alarmin and stromal pathways) are emerging but earlier; none yet displaces the TL1A+IL-23 rationale, though they belong on the watch list as the field expands.

Sizing the prize and the threat (TD Cowen). IBD therapeutics are ~$30.7B (2024) growing to ~$41.9B (2030), with AbbVie extending from ~31% to ~44% share via Rinvoq + Skyrizi. Rinvoq (oral JAK) alone is projected at ~$3.7B in IBD by 2027, already capturing ~50% of new Crohn's and ~33% of new UC starts — the oral is taking major share now, which sets the efficacy/durability bar a quarterly injectable must beat.

6 · Safety & immunogenicity

  • IL-23p19 arm: the approved class (risankizumab, mirikizumab, guselkumab) shows low serious-infection rates and no malignancy signal — cleaner than anti-TNF or JAK.

  • Immunogenicity is the bispecific battleground. Spyre management has publicly characterized first-generation TL1A bispecifics as showing unfavorable immunogenicity, while XmAb942 (anti-TL1A) reports no anti-drug antibodies to date. Clean immunogenicity is therefore both a key technical risk for a bispecific and a concrete, named differentiation target.

  • TL1A arm: through 50 weeks in ARTEMIS-UC, tulisokibart showed no new safety signals — infections upper-respiratory-predominant (37%/46% at Wk50), no serious infections, no infection discontinuations.

  • The open question: whether simultaneous TL1A + IL-23 blockade raises infection risk beyond either alone — no human dual-blockade dataset exists yet; it is a primary focus of a partner's diligence.

8 · Payer differentiation

Payers set realized value; they decide which agent is reimbursed and at what step. What moves a payer in IBD:

LeverWhat payers reward
Step therapyanti-TNF biosimilar is required first on cost; a new agent must clear that hurdle and justify a premium over a generic-priced incumbent.
Efficacy barminimum ~>30-35% clinical remission vs placebo; preferred placement needs >50% at Week 52 with mucosal healing — i.e., durable deep remission, not just induction.
Comparative effectivenessbenchmarked against risankizumab/mirikizumab (IL-23p19) and upadacitinib; a bispecific must show a remission or durability edge to win formulary preference.
Dosing & site of careSC Q8-Q12W with a prefilled pen and home self-injection lowers total cost vs IV infusion (site-of-care fees) — a real economic differentiator.
Line placementvalue is highest if the agent earns earlier placement; payers gate this on head-to-head or strong indirect-comparison evidence.

Price anchors (Wedbush Rx tracker). The IL-23 class lists at roughly $7.5-10k/month — Skyrizi ~$7,488, Tremfya ~$7,646, Stelara ~$8,700, Omvoh ~$9,946; S1P orals are cheaper (etrasimod ~$6,123, ozanimod ~$9,609), anti-TNF ~$6-7k (Humira ~$7,271). A new agent is priced into this band and judged on net cost after rebates. A Spyre patient survey found a ~60/40 preference for a quarterly injectable over a once-daily oral in IBD — a data point that supports the SC-bispecific value case on both cost-of-care and patient preference.

Implication. A TL1A×IL-23 bispecific's payer case is built on durable deep remission in the refractory population plus an SC convenience/cost advantage over IV incumbents — and it must answer the comparison to cheap anti-TNF biosimilars and convenient orals, not only to other TL1A assets.

10 · Competitive geography

The TL1A×IL-23 bispecific field is disproportionately China-originated — LaNova (LQ080, out-licensed ex-China to Zymeworks), Simcere (SIM0709, to Boehringer Ingelheim), plus NovaMab, Elpiscience, Biocad and Qyuns. The recurring deal pattern is a Western pharma licensing-in a China-originated bispecific. Competitive sets and likely acquirers are global; Western-quality data packages and clear IP convert a China-originated molecule into a premium transaction.

The value-destroying case. If the first bispecific reads show no additive efficacy and ATLAS-UC succeeds (tulisokibart becomes standard of care), a bispecific that does not beat a single antibody has no buyer, and a preclinical entrant's value approaches zero. The defense is a clinical design that reads additive efficacy early and cheaply (a focused Ph1b), so a program can stop or pivot before committing Phase 2 capital.
