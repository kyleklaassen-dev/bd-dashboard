The landscape in brief

Thyroid eye disease has exactly one approved drug — teprotumumab (anti-IGF-1R, IV) — and a fast-filling pipeline behind it. Veligrotug (Viridian, IV) is at the FDA with a June 2026 decision; subcutaneous anti-IGF-1R agents (elegrobart, lonigutamab) and an oral (linsitinib) are advancing on convenience. Every clinical asset blocks the same effector receptor, IGF-1R. The strategic questions are format (replacing IV infusions with SC or oral), safety (reducing the hearing-loss and hyperglycemia burden), and whether a next-generation agent can also engage the upstream autoimmune driver, TSHR, for deeper or more durable disease control. No IGF-1R × TSHR dual agent has clinical data; the dual thesis is the least clinically established of the Meridian areas and is flagged as such.

State of validation: IGF-1R blockade is approved and highly effective (proptosis response ~70-83%); the format is shifting IV→SC/oral; TSHR-directed and dual IGF-1R×TSHR approaches remain early/hypothetical. The near-term value is convenience and safety differentiation on a proven target; the longer-term thesis is adding the autoimmune (TSHR) arm.
This is a hypothesis-driven area — read it that way. Unlike the other Meridian dual theses, no clinical dataset currently shows that adding TSHR engagement improves outcomes beyond IGF-1R blockade. IGF-1R is validated and the bar (teprotumumab) is very high; differentiation here is mostly format and safety on a proven target, not a new mechanism. The dual IGF-1R×TSHR concept is a mechanistic hypothesis, not a validated strategy, and is treated as such throughout.

Field view. TED went from no approved drug to a one-product, multi-billion-dollar market with teprotumumab and triggered Amgen's ~$27.8B acquisition of Horizon. The field is now an IGF-1R format race — IV → SC → oral — with safety (hearing, glucose) as the second differentiator. The unexploited frontier is the disease's autoimmune root: TSHR. Whether engaging TSHR alongside IGF-1R deepens or durably extends response is the open scientific question and the rationale for a dual agent.

1 · Patient & disease

Thyroid eye disease is the orbital manifestation of Graves' autoimmunity: TSHR autoantibodies, acting through the IGF-1R/TSHR complex on orbital fibroblasts, drive tissue expansion, inflammation and scarring — producing proptosis, double vision, pain and, in the worst cases, optic-nerve compression and vision loss. Most TED is mild; ~20-30% is moderate-to-severe and 3-5% sight-threatening, giving roughly 50,000-100,000 treatable US patients. The disease has an active (inflammatory) phase and a chronic (fibrotic) phase, historically managed with steroids, orbital radiation and decompression surgery before teprotumumab.

The patient, not just the disease. TED is disfiguring and frightening — bulging eyes, constant double vision, the threat of blindness — and falls on a mostly working-age, predominantly female population. 'Better,' from their side, is reduced proptosis and resolved double vision, achieved without 8 infusion-center visits, without new hearing loss or blood-sugar problems, and ideally in chronic as well as active disease. Those experiential gaps — convenience and the hearing/glycemic AEs — are exactly where the next generation of IGF-1R agents competes.

Why a dual (IGF-1R × TSHR) concept. Every approved and late-stage agent blocks IGF-1R, the effector. The disease is driven upstream by TSHR autoantibodies, which current drugs do not touch. The sharpest commercial hypothesis is therefore narrow: can engaging TSHR — the autoimmune driver — enable durable benefit in chronic (fibrotic) TED, where effector-only blockade is less effective? (Deepening the acute response is a secondary, lower-value possibility, since IGF-1R blockade may already capture most of the clinically relevant signaling.) This is mechanistically reasonable but clinically unproven — the most speculative of the Meridian dual theses, and labelled a hypothesis throughout.

3 · The scientific thesis

The targets, precisely

TED has a clean two-node biology. IGF-1R (the IGF1R gene product) on orbital fibroblasts is the effector: its blockade shrinks orbital tissue and resolves proptosis — the mechanism of every approved and late-stage agent. TSHR (the thyroid-stimulating-hormone receptor) is the autoantigen: Graves' stimulating autoantibodies activate it, and IGF-1R and TSHR physically and functionally crosstalk on the orbital fibroblast, so IGF-1R blockade partly interrupts TSHR-driven signaling. On-target ceiling: IGF-1R has metabolic and auditory roles, which is why the class causes hyperglycemia and hearing impairment — the safety ceiling a next-generation agent must lower.

What is proven, and the open frontier

Effector blockade is decisively validated: teprotumumab produced ~83% proptosis response and veligrotug 70% — among the largest effect sizes in immunology. What is not addressed is the upstream autoimmune driver (TSHR) or the chronic/fibrotic phase. The near-term innovation is therefore format and safety on the same proven target (SC/oral, less hearing/glucose toxicity); the longer-term, higher-risk innovation is engaging TSHR to reach durability and chronic disease — a hypothesis the IGF-1R/TSHR crosstalk biology supports but no clinical data yet confirm.

Testable conclusion. Near-term: an SC or oral IGF-1R agent that matches teprotumumab's proptosis response with less hearing/glycemic toxicity and no infusions wins on convenience and safety. Longer-term and unproven: if TSHR engagement adds to IGF-1R blockade, a dual agent could deepen or durably extend response and reach chronic TED — falsified if TSHR targeting adds no benefit over IGF-1R alone. No dual IGF-1R×TSHR clinical data exist; this is the area's principal scientific unknown.

Why this matters. TED competition is, for now, entirely within IGF-1R — so differentiation is format and safety, not target. The first agent to deliver teprotumumab-level proptosis response by self-injection or pill, with less hearing/glycemic toxicity, takes share in a small but high-value market. The only way to escape the IGF-1R commoditization is a genuinely new mechanism (TSHR / autoimmune), which remains early.

Beyond active TED. IGF-1R and the Graves' autoimmune axis extend to chronic TED, and the broader Graves' disease and autoimmune-thyroid space — label-expansion optionality (chronic disease, earlier intervention) that a safer, more convenient, or TSHR-engaging agent could capture beyond the active-moderate-severe population.

6 · Safety & the differentiation that matters

  • Hearing impairment is the signature risk. Teprotumumab causes hearing loss/tinnitus in a meaningful fraction of patients — a serious, sometimes irreversible AE that limits use and is a primary target for next-generation differentiation.

  • Hyperglycemia. IGF-1R's metabolic role drives hyperglycemia, requiring monitoring especially in diabetic patients; a cleaner metabolic profile is a real clinical and payer advantage.

  • Format is a safety/experience issue too. Eight IV infusions impose burden and infusion reactions; SC or oral dosing improves the experience and may broaden the prescriber base beyond infusion-capable sites.

  • A dual TSHR arm adds biologic uncertainty, not just thyroid-axis risk. Engaging TSHR raises three distinct concerns an IGF-1R-only agent does not carry: receptor-agonism risk (a TSHR antibody could inadvertently stimulate rather than block the receptor, worsening Graves' hyperthyroidism), receptor-modulation complexity (partial agonism/antagonism is hard to engineer and predict), and autoimmune amplification (perturbing the autoantigen could alter the underlying autoimmune response). These are why the dual concept is materially higher-risk than format/safety differentiation on IGF-1R alone.

10 · Competitive geography

TED development is led by US biotech (Viridian; Alumis, which absorbed ACELYRIN in 2025; Sling) under Amgen's incumbent franchise — a Western-concentrated field, in contrast to the China-heavy bispecific supply in IBD and respiratory. The likely transactions are US-biotech takeouts (Viridian the cleanest) or format-defense moves by Amgen. China-origin IGF-1R and TSHR programs are emerging but earlier; they are the next wave rather than today's competitive set.

The value-destroying case. The defense is clear differentiation (format, safety, or a proven new mechanism) before committing to a high-cost specialist launch.
